# Claktory manual

Claktory is where a person's agents live and work: persistent agents, each with its own name, prompt, model and folder, on the person's own machine or server. This manual covers what Claktory can do and how, in the app and as an agent.

## What's in the app

<!-- admin: app-claktories | The rail of Claktories at the window's left: several connected at once, switching, adding, naming, coloring and removing one; the phone's list. -->
### Claktories
- The app can be connected to several Claktories at once: this Mac's, and others on other machines (a Linux server, another Mac), each paired with this device. All of them stay connected and live; nothing is shared or merged between them, and the window shows one at a time.
- Each Claktory has a name and a color of its own, the same on every device that connects to it: its name starts as its computer's name (a Mac's Computer Name, from System Settings → General → Sharing, or the host name elsewhere), and its color is picked for it, different from one Claktory to the next.
- A thin rail at the window's far left has a tile for each, on a wash of its color: this Mac's (a laptop glyph) first, then the others in the order they were added, each with its name's initials ("mac-mini" is "MM") in its color. The one showing is tinted more, with an orange mark at the window's edge. Click another to switch the whole window to it at once, as it was left. Hovering one shows its name, whether it's connected and how (directly, or by an indirect route that may be a little slower), and since when.
- At a glance: a tile waves softly while one of its agents works, has a dot at its corner while one has something new (orange while something there waits for your answer), a pulsing orange dot while it's connecting or reconnecting, and a red dot when it can't connect or doesn't allow this device. A Claktory that isn't connected yet is faint.
- **+** at the bottom of the rail adds one: paste the pairing code made for this device on that Claktory (Settings → Devices → Add a device…), and it connects and shows.
- Right-click a tile to change its name or color (for every device; an empty name goes back to its computer's name; only while it's connected) or, for one on another machine, **Remove…** it: this device disconnects, forgets its code and leaves that Claktory's devices. Its agents and work stay there.
- On iPhone, the conversation fills the screen and the **Menu** button at its top left (or a swipe from the screen's left edge) opens a drawer: the Claktories down its left side, then the one showing, with its name, its connection in a word ("Connected", "Reconnecting…", "Can't connect"; tap it for more and **Reconnect**), a search field that narrows the list to the agents and pipes whose name, role or project matches, **Needs you** (the agents and pipes waiting for an answer; **See all** opens everything waiting on one page, to answer there), and its projects, agents and pipes, with Map, Usage, Marketplace, Settings and What's new at the end. Tap another Claktory to see its agents (the drawer stays open), and an agent or pipe to open its conversation; swipe the drawer back, or tap the dimmed conversation, to close it. Each Claktory's tile shows its news and connection; a long press on one changes its name or color, or removes it, and **+** pairs another by scanning or pasting its code. At the largest text sizes a **Claktories** row opens the list of them instead, and over a first-launch step "‹ Claktories" at the top goes to that list.
- On iPhone, buttons, fields, switches and dropdowns are sized for a finger (at least 44 points, their text at iOS's 17 points, growing with the system's text size), a dropdown's choices open in a sheet, text in a field selects the iPhone's way (handles, magnifier, the system's Copy and Paste menu), and what a Mac shows in a dialog rises from the bottom as a sheet: drag it down, or tap above it, to close it.

### Sidebar
- Drag the sidebar's right edge to make it narrower or a little wider (200 to 300 points; 248 by default); double-click the edge to go back to the default. The width is kept in Claktory, so it's the same on every device.
- At the top, on their own, are the agents that aren't in any project (the workspace's agents), such as Rivet.
- **Projects** lists projects (folder icon), each with its agents underneath. New agents always go in a project. A project can have **folders** (such as "App" for its code and "Research" for write-ups): its agents that work in the project's own folder come first, then a quiet heading for each folder with its agents under it. Click a heading to fold its agents away (it then shows how many it hides, and the wave while one works); drop an agent on a heading to put it in that folder.
- New projects and agents are set up by Rivet, which picks the prompt, model and extensions that suit what {{user}} wants. The small "+" by **Projects**, and the one on a project's row while the pointer is on it (or on the focused project's name), open Rivet's conversation with the request started in its message box, "Set up a new project for " or "Add an agent to <project> that ", for {{user}} to finish and send; anything already typed to Rivet stays, with the request on a new line after it. Hovering the "+" says "Ask Rivet to set it up". When there's no Rivet, or it has no model available, the "+" opens the New project or New agent form instead.
- Each agent shows as its character: a small, living shape in the agent's color (circle, square, triangle, diamond, pentagon or hexagon) with the same two eyes as every agent. Claktory picks the shape from the agent's id unless one is chosen in its settings, so renaming or moving it never changes it; Rivet starts as a blue hexagon. Its eyes show its mood: idle, glancing about and blinking now and then; thinking, looking up; running a tool, looking down at the work; something new to see, pleased; its last run failed, eyes down; no model, half-lidded; asleep after 15 minutes idle, eyes closed. The same character, smaller and resting, marks the agent wherever it's named: beside its messages, as an owner, in pickers. With reduced motion every face holds still in its mood. While the agent works, a soft wave of its color sweeps across its row, again and again, until the run ends (with reduced motion, the row is tinted in its color instead); a thin light runs along its header's colored line too.
- An agent's row shows its name and, when it has one, its **role** under it in smaller, fainter type ("Mike" over "Coordinator"); a row with a role is a little taller. The role shows the same way in the agent's header and in the Messaging panel, and faintly after the name over its messages and in pickers. Set it in the agent's settings, or when creating it with the New agent form.
- When an agent finishes (or fails) while you're looking elsewhere, its name turns bold and bright, with a dot in its color at the end of the row: something happened. Opening the agent clears it. Stopping an agent yourself doesn't mark it.
- When something in an agent's conversation waits for {{user}}'s answer (a question it can't go on without, a secret it asked for, a service it proposes to connect, a message it proposes for Rivet, a secret it wants to use after reading a pipe), its row ends with an orange badge saying how many, in place of the dot, until {{user}} answers. Clicking the agent opens its conversation at the oldest one when it's news (among the latest messages, with nothing from {{user}} after it); otherwise the conversation opens at its end as usual, and clicking the badge itself goes to the oldest one, however far back. A collapsed agent, a folded folder and, in a focused project, "‹ All projects" show the badge for what they hide.
- **Needs you**, at the top of the sidebar while anything waits for {{user}}'s answer, gathers it all in one place with how many: every agent's and chat's waiting cards and every pipe's approval, held messages and loop pause. Click it to open the Needs you page beside the conversation (on iPhone, from the drawer). Each item says who asks and what, newest first; click it to open the card itself and answer it right there, with the same controls as in the conversation, or **Open conversation** (**Open chat**, **Open pipe**) to go to it. What {{user}} answers stays on the page, marked Answered, until they leave it. On a Mac, a chip at the top right of every conversation ("2 need you") says the same while anything waits; click it to drop the items down over the conversation and answer them there. A proposal to Rivet has **Dismiss** on its item, folded, so a stale one goes in one click.
- An agent's question is a card in its conversation: what it needs and why, and either a box for {{user}}'s answer with **Answer**, or **Approve** with an optional comment when it only needs a yes. **Dismiss** leaves it to the agent's judgment. Its text can be selected and copied, and a command in it has **Copy** beside it.
- Drag an agent onto a project to move it there, at the top level; drop it on the edge of a workspace agent's row (such as Rivet's) to move it beside it, out of any project. Its conversation moves with it, and so do its reports.
- The sidebar shows who reports to whom: an agent's reports (the agents it leads in its project) sit indented under it; an agent that reports to an admin agent (such as Rivet) stays at its project's top level. Arrange agents by dragging: drop one on the middle of another agent's row (the row is outlined) to make it that agent's report; drop it on the upper or lower edge of a row (a line shows where) to put it there: among its own level, that only changes the order; at another level, it reports to that level's lead (or to no one, at the top). While a drop would change its lead, the dragged name says so ("Reports to Mike", "Reports to no one"). On iPhone, hold an agent still a moment in the drawer, then drag. If Claktory refuses a lead (one that would make a loop, say), it says why. The arrow beside an agent with reports collapses or expands them. A collapsed agent shows the wave while one of its hidden agents works, and shows the unseen marker when one has something new. The order, leads and collapsing are kept by Claktory, the same on every device, and a lead set in an agent's settings or by Rivet shows here too.
- Click a project to focus it (see below). Right-click a project (on a phone, tap its ⋯ or touch and hold it, and choose Project settings) for its settings: rename it, archive it (**Archive project**, at once: it and its agents leave the sidebar and the rail's marks, and nothing is deleted), delete it, and keep its folders (rename one, point it at another path, remove it, or "Add a folder…", which asks Rivet to set it up, or opens a short form when Rivet can't run). Removing a folder moves its agents to the project's folder; no files are deleted.
- Archived projects are listed under **Archived (N)** at the bottom of the project list: click it to open the list, then **Unarchive** on one to bring it back (or click it for its settings). An archived project's agent opened from a link says so above its message box, with **Unarchive**.
- **Chats**, under the projects, lists {{user}}'s 20 most recent chats (a speech bubble and the title; bold with a dot when it replied while elsewhere, an orange badge while a proposal to Rivet waits), then **Show all** for every chat, with a search by title (on iPhone, in the drawer, with **See all**). Its "+" (or **New chat** while there are none) opens an empty chat at once; one left without a message is dropped. A chat opens in place of an agent's conversation: a plain bar with its title and model, the conversation, and a box for text only (no files or voice yet). Esc or **Stop** stops a reply. Click the title to rename it (left empty, Claktory names it again); its **…**, a right click on its row or touching and holding it on iPhone offer **Rename…** and **Delete…**, which asks first and can't be undone. A proposal to Rivet in a chat is approved or dismissed on its card, or from Needs you.
- At the bottom, the cog opens **Settings**, the bag opens the **Marketplace**, the bar chart opens **Usage** (tokens by agent, provider and model; see Usage), the scroll opens **What's new**, and your avatar at the end of the row opens Settings, where your profile is (see Settings). What's new is every version of Claktory, newest first, on a timeline, each with its date and what it added, changed and fixed; the one running is marked "This version", and any not installed yet "Update to get it". On a Mac each opens as a pane right of the selected agent's conversation (see Panes), so the conversation stays readable beside it; its button shows it's open, and clicking it again closes the pane. One already open glows instead of opening twice. With no agent selected it takes the whole view, with Done (or Esc) to go back; on an iPhone it's a page over the conversation, with Back (or a swipe from the left edge) to go back. The footer also shows a sign-in in progress, and "Settings file has an error" when the settings file is broken. Clicking that offers "Restore last working settings", and **Tell Rivet**. Above the version, always, a line with a dot says how the app is connected to the Claktory it shows: "Running on this Mac" or "Connected to mac-mini" (a faint dot), "Reconnecting…" or "Connecting to mac-mini…" (a pulsing orange dot), "Can't connect to mac-mini" (red). Hovering it (or touching and holding it) says how it connects (directly, or by an indirect route that may be a little slower) and since when; clicking it opens the same with **Reconnect**, which connects afresh at once, the reason when it can't connect, and **Switch Claktory…**. Claktory's version is shown under it in small type; it's only text: the rail at the window's left (on an iPhone, the list of Claktories) switches and adds them. When a Claktory doesn't accept this device (it was removed, or its code was used by another device), the line says "<machine> doesn't allow this device" and stops trying; clicking it lists the Claktories this device knows: **This Mac**, and each one on another machine it has connected to (hover one to Forget it); click one to show it. **Connect to another Claktory** there takes the pairing code made for this device on that Claktory (see Settings → Devices) and connects.
- Wherever Claktory shows an error (a broken settings file, a run that failed, an update that didn't work or was undone, a request it refused, a sign-in that failed, remote access, a folder Files can't read, a failed monitor), **Tell Rivet** beside it opens Rivet's conversation with the error typed in its message box, ready to send, so Rivet can look into it and fix it. It shows only while Rivet can run. A run that failed because the provider refused the agent's key or sign-in says so instead ("Anthropic refused Rivet's key.") with the way to fix it: **Change key…** or **Sign in again…** starts that sign-in in Settings → Providers, replacing the refused account (**Add a key…** for a key from the environment, which can't be replaced, adds one before it).
- Text you read can be selected anywhere: the conversation (tool calls' input and output too), Claktory's notes, the panels, Settings, What's new, the Marketplace, dialogs and errors. Buttons, the sidebar and other things you click aren't. A right click selects the word under it (or keeps what's selected) and opens a menu with **Copy**; ⌘C copies too. In a conversation the menu also has **Reply**, which quotes the message under the pointer into the message box, ready for {{user}}'s answer; on iPhone, touch and hold a message and choose **Reply**. A message from another agent is quoted with its sender's name ("Kenji wrote:"). Copied text keeps its shape: a blank line between paragraphs, each list item on its own line with its bullet or number, nested items indented under theirs, and a table's cells separated by tabs.
- Just above the cog and the bag, a small circle for each provider Claktory is signed in to (for a provider with several subscription accounts, one per account), in that provider's color with its product's logo inside (Claude's for Anthropic, OpenAI's for OpenAI and Codex, OpenRouter's). They wrap onto more rows as there are more. For a subscription the ring around the logo fills as the weekly limit is used: empty at 0%, full at 100%. Hovering it shows the plan (such as "Claude Max 20x" or "ChatGPT Pro"; until it's known, how it's signed in), every limit's percentage used and when it resets, and when the numbers were last read; right after signing in it says the usage isn't read yet. If they couldn't be read, it says why and shows the last ones it had (the circle is faded); a dashed ring means there are no numbers yet. A provider signed in with an API key has no limits, so it shows just its logo, with no ring. A subscription with usage resets left (Claude's 5-hour, weekly, full and Fable resets, ChatGPT's reset credits) lists them at the end of its card, each with how many are left and when they must be used by, and **Use…**: it asks first, then uses it and says how it went, with a link to the provider's own page when the provider refused Claktory. Nothing ever uses one on its own, and no agent can. A small dot on the circle means a limit one of its resets refills is 90% used or more. The card stays open while the pointer is on it; on iPhone, touch and hold the circle to open it.

### A focused project, and its files
- Clicking a project focuses it: the sidebar shows only that project and its agents, with Rivet kept at the top so it's always a click away (it's the one who changes the project for you; it's there whenever there's an admin agent), and "‹ All projects" goes back to everything. Click (or right-click) the project's name at the top to rename or delete it. A new project opens focused.
- **Files:** the file button on a project's row while the pointer is on it (and on the focused project's name) opens the project's Files as a pane right of the selected agent's conversation, focused or not; Files already open glow instead, and the button stays visible while they're open. On iPhone, tap a project's ⋯ in the drawer (or touch and hold it) and choose Files: they open as a page over the conversation, a file opens over them, and Back (or a swipe from the left edge) goes back. Files show the project's folder, the same one its agents work in, and update as they change files. Each of the project's folders follows as a section of its own, headed by its name and path; the project's own folder steps aside when it's empty and the project has folders. No directory shows twice: a folder that is the project's own folder, or inside it, shows only as its own section. After them, each agent here with its own checkout (the Own checkout extension) has a section too, "Builder's checkout", with its branch and path, showing the files as that agent has them. Click a folder to open or close it; click a file to open it in a viewer right of the Files (the next ones stack under it), or make its pane glow when it's open. Which folders are open, and Show hidden files, are kept per project, the same on every device. Files are a pane like any other: move, resize or close them.
- Files hide what the folder's `.factoryignore` lists (same syntax as `.gitignore`), such as `node_modules/` and build output. New projects get one with common entries; a folder without one hides those same entries. "Show hidden files" shows them too, dimmed.

### Updates
- Versions are numbered v0.1.0, v0.1.1, v0.1.2…, one up with every release; the middle number goes up (v0.2.0) when a new app and an older backend can't work together, so both update. Versions before v0.1.0 were named by date (2026.11.19-1958).
- An app and a Claktory whose middle numbers differ can't work together, so the app shows only which to update, over everything but the update banner and the rail of Claktories: "Update Claktory to use this app" when Claktory is older, "This app is too old for this Claktory" when the app is, each with both versions and how to update (Update in the banner when the latest release brings them together; otherwise on that Claktory's machine, or the iPhone app from where it was installed). What's new still opens over it.
- When a new version of Claktory is published, a banner at the top says so, with an Update button, and "What's new" to see what the update brings. It can't be dismissed. A Claktory installed with the online installer checks for new versions shortly after it starts and every half hour, and downloads one before offering it; nothing is installed until you click Update. It takes a new version only when Claktory's own release key signed it, and on a Mac only when Claktory's developer signed its app, so no one else can pass off an update as Claktory's, even if the place it's downloaded from were taken over; one that isn't signed so is never offered. (A channel in a folder, for trying a release out, is taken unsigned.)
- Updating restarts the app, and Claktory's backend when the release changed it. Agents that were working pick up where they left off, told that Claktory restarted; messages waiting for them are kept. A release that only changed the app doesn't restart the backend at all.
- If a new backend doesn't start, Claktory goes back to the one before and the banner says what happened, with "Try again".
- On a Mac, the first update that finds Claktory's launch settings older than a new install's (`~/Library/LaunchAgents/dev.factory.factoryd.plist`; today: running as an interactive program, so macOS doesn't throttle it and what agents run) brings them up to date once, about a minute after it starts, and restarts the backend once more to apply them. If the updated settings don't load, the old ones are put back. What it did is in `{{home}}/bin/launch-agent.log`.
- On a Claktory on another machine, Update first brings that version's app over from it: the banner says how far it is ("Getting the app ready on mac-mini…", then how much has downloaded, then "Checking the new app…"). The app is replaced only once it checks out (its size and checksum, that it's Claktory, and its signature); the app then quits and opens again as the new version. If anything goes wrong, the banner says why, with "Try again", and nothing has changed.

### Developer Tools (faster builds on a Mac)
- macOS checks every new program the first time it runs, and agents build and run many (each test binary a build makes): a fraction of a second each, which adds up to most of a minute on a large test run. A program macOS counts as a developer tool spares everything it starts these checks.
- What agents run is started by Claktory's supervisor, which on a Mac installed (or reinstalled) with this version's installer is an app of its own, **Claktory Agents**, in `{{home}}/bin/Claktory Agents.app`. Installs from before run a bare program there instead, which macOS can't list; reinstalling Claktory (its installer again, over the installed Claktory) brings the app. Updates never change the supervisor.
- To add it: in Terminal, `spctl developer-mode enable-terminal` (once, so System Settings shows the Developer Tools list); then System Settings → Privacy & Security → Developer Tools → **+**, press ⇧⌘G, enter `{{home}}/bin/Claktory Agents.app`, click Open, and make sure it's switched on.
- To check it: have an agent run `cd "$(mktemp -d)" && printf 'int main(void){return 0;}' > t.c && cc -o t t.c && time ./t`. Without the grant the first run of a new program takes about 0.1–0.2 s; with it, a few milliseconds.
- Until Claktory's releases are signed with its Developer ID, macOS knows Claktory Agents by its exact build, so after a reinstall it needs adding again (remove the old entry with **−** first).

### Agent view (click an agent)
- The header is tinted with the agent's color and shows its character, name (with its role under it), model and effort, and a meter of its context: tokens used out of the compaction threshold. Clicking the header opens the agent's settings: its character (a live preview, with each mood to try, then its shape, Automatic or one picked), name, role (optional; empty for none), color, prompt (Rivet's says whether it's Claktory's default, and once changed, **Reset to default** puts the default back), model, effort, **Fallback model** (what a step its model declines is tried again on; **Default** unless one is picked), project, its folder (when the project has folders: the project's own folder or one of them), its **Lead** (the agent it reports to, which sees how it's set up: one in its project, or an admin agent; None for none), its message box hint (what the empty message box says; left empty, "Message <name>"; several lines, one example per line, take turns, each sliding up as the next comes in, and hold still while the pointer is over the box), its **Secrets** (a switch for each of the vault's secrets, on for those it has; the Secrets extension's box names them too), its extensions, and "Delete agent…". Under **Extensions**, a switch turns each extension on or off for this agent, and while it's on, its options show underneath: more switches, or a list of agents (click it to choose agents from other projects, grouped by project; agents in its own project are already included, shown checked and greyed). Changes apply with Save.
- If the agent's model can't run because its provider isn't signed in, the header says "No model available", with a "Sign in…" button that opens Settings at Providers.
- The message box: Enter sends, Shift+Enter adds a line. What's typed and not sent stays as that agent's draft, through switching agents, opening Settings or the Marketplace, and restarting or updating the app, on this device; sending (or a command that runs) clears it. A message shows in the conversation the moment it's sent, labeled "Sending…" until Claktory has it; one that can't be sent (the connection dropped) stays there with "Not sent", Retry, and Edit to take it back to the box. A message sent while the agent is working waits only until the agent's current step ends (a model reply, or a tool call such as a command finishing), then the agent reads it before going on. While it waits, it shows at the end of the conversation as its bubble, faded and labeled "Queued" (messages from other agents waiting for it show the same way, on the left); when the agent takes it, it turns into the normal bubble in place. The sidebar shows how many are waiting. Stop (or Esc) stops the run; what's queued stays, and the agent gets it right after, as a new run. A message from you also moves a long command the agent is running to the background (another agent's, once the command has run a minute), so it's read sooner.
- Files: drag files onto the window (Mac: the whole window dims and says whose message they'll go to, or why they can't go there, as in a chat, which takes text only, or with no conversation open), paste them or a picture with ⌘V, or use the paperclip at the end of the message box (on iPhone: Choose from Photos, Choose from Files or Paste image); they wait in the box as chips (× takes one out) and go with the next message, which may have no text. Sending uploads them to the agent's uploads folder, with progress on each, and the agent gets them with the message; a cut-off upload picks up where it stopped on Retry. In the conversation a picture shows as a thumbnail and any other file as a chip; a click opens it in a viewer beside the conversation.
- Voice messages (Mac): the mic at the end of the message box records one; the box shows a live waveform, the time and Cancel, and the mic becomes Send (Return sends too, Esc cancels). Recording stops by itself at 10 minutes and sends. The first time on a Claktory without its speech model, it asks before downloading it (about 740 MB, on the computer Claktory runs on) and starts the download while you record. The message shows at once as a bubble with its length, labeled "Transcribing…" ("Downloading the speech model… 312 of 740 MB" while the model downloads); the computer Claktory runs on turns it into text, so the recording goes nowhere else, and the agent gets the words as your message, in order with what you send after it. Then the bubble shows what was heard, with ▶ and the length to play the recording. One that can't be transcribed says why, with Retry and Delete. Where the computer can't recognize speech, the mic is greyed out and says why. Settings → Voice shows the speech model (downloading, ready, or why it failed), with Download, Stop, Try again or Remove, and its licence's credit. The Mac asks once for the microphone; if it's refused, the box says where to allow it.
- Slash commands: typing "/" at the start of the message box lists the agent's commands, filtered as you type; arrows pick one, Enter (or a click) runs it, Tab completes its name to add arguments, Esc closes the list. **/clear** starts a new conversation (the old one is archived; its checklist and memory stay). **/compact** summarizes the conversation now and carries on from the summary; it can't run while the agent is working. If a command is refused, the reason shows above the box. Text starting with "/" that isn't a command is sent as a message.
- {{User}}'s messages are bubbles on the right in the agent's color; the agent's own replies are bubbles on the left, tinted with its color and edged in it, with no name over them (another agent's messages stay filled with that agent's color, under its name). When the agent answers one of {{user}}'s messages with others in between, its reply shows a quote of it on top ("You" and the message's first words); clicking it goes to that message. The conversation grows with the window, up to about twice a comfortable reading width. A long conversation opens at its latest messages, and earlier ones load as {{user}} scrolls up. Scrolled up, a button at the bottom right (on iPhone, Latest at the foot) goes back down to the latest and follows it again, saying how many replies came in meanwhile ("3 new"). It also shows the agent's replies, its thinking, its tool calls (the calls one reply made share a block; each says on its row what it does, such as the command it runs with "+N lines" when there's more or the file it reads, and at the end how long it has run, ticking (for a command, also when it times out: "1m 23s · times out in 8m 37s", or "no limit" in the background), and once it's done, how long it took; click a call to expand its input and output), compaction dividers, and notices such as errors. An extension's tool calls show what they did with its icon, in a line and the details under it: a message sent ("→" the recipient, then the message, and Sent, or Queued when the recipient was working), the checklist items added or updated with their status and owner, a note read or remembered with its text, a monitor's name and when it fires, a background task's command and whether it still runs, the file shown, a search's results and the page read, a database query's SQL and row count, a secret given to agents or taken back, and the error when one failed. A message, a note or a monitor's prompt shows its first line, with Show more to read it all. The recipient opens their conversation, the file brings its pane forward, and a result or page opens in the browser.
- An agent's proposal to Rivet shows in its conversation as a card in Rivet's color: the proposal, a box for a comment, and **Approve** (or Enter in the box) and **Dismiss**. Approved, Rivet gets your comment and the proposal as your message; if it can't (no Rivet, or Rivet has no model), why shows on the card and the proposal waits. Decided, the card says Approved or Dismissed, when, and your comment.

## Agents

- An agent is persistent: one conversation that continues across messages, days and restarts.
- Every agent is the same coding agent. What sets one apart is its **name** (often a human first name, such as "Mike"), its **role** (what it does, such as "Coordinator", shown with the name; optional), its **prompt** (instructions added to its system prompt), its **model**, its **effort** (how hard the model reasons) and its **project**.
<!-- admin: user-name | The person's first name: where it's set, and how agents' prompts and Claktory's texts use it. -->
- **The user's name:** the person agents work for can give their first name (asked when the app first opens, and in Settings). Every agent's system prompt then names them ("The person you work with is Lee."), and Claktory's own texts and events use it ("Lee approved your proposal to Rivet"); without it, they say "the person". A new name reaches agents already working as a short note from Claktory, and their system prompt says it from their next context.
<!-- admin: prompt-placeholders | Placeholders an agent's prompt can use for the person's name and Claktory's folders. -->
- An agent's prompt can use the manual's placeholders: `{{user}}` and `{{User}}` become {{user}}'s name (or "the person" and "The person" without one), and `{{home}}`, `{{config}}` and `{{workspace}}` this Claktory's folders. They're filled in when its system prompt is written, and the prompt keeps them as written.
<!-- admin: choosing-models | Which kind of model suits which job. -->
- Models suit different jobs: a strong reasoning model for building software, another for writing that should sound human. Each agent picks its own.
<!-- admin: agent-colors | Agent colors: the eight, and how a new agent's is picked. -->
- Every agent has a **color**: blue, violet, green, pink, teal, amber, red or slate. Unless {{user}} picks one, a new agent gets the color that looks least like any agent's color, so agents are as easy to tell apart as possible (after Rivet's blue: red, green, slate, pink…). Once all eight are taken, the one fewest agents have.
- A change to an agent's model, effort, prompt, name, role, project or folder applies from its next step (its next model call), even mid-task, and Claktory tells the agent in a short note ("Your model is now …").
- An agent works in its project's folder, or in the workspace folder `{{workspace}}` when it's in no project. Its tools read, write and edit files and run shell commands; one whose model takes image input also looks at image files with `read_image` (an image too big for the model is scaled down for it).
- In a project with **folders** (see Projects), an agent works in one of them or in the project's own folder: its shell starts there, and write_file and edit_file change files only there, though it can read the whole project. bash can't be held to its folder, so the agent changes files only in its own with bash too. Its system prompt lists the project's folders and which one is its own.
- When an agent's context reaches the compaction threshold (400,000 tokens unless changed), Claktory summarizes the conversation and the agent continues from the summary; a divider marks the spot. An idle agent with a long conversation can also be summarized just before its model's prompt cache expires.
<!-- admin: idle-summary | Summarizing idle conversations before the prompt cache expires: when, for which providers, and the settings. -->
- An agent left idle with a long conversation is also summarized just before its model's prompt cache expires, so {{user}}'s next message after a long break doesn't pay to cache the whole conversation again: after 54 minutes when Anthropic's "Keep conversations ready" (Settings → Providers) is 1 hour, or after 4 when it's 5 minutes (the default) and "Also after 5 minutes idle" is on. Only for a conversation of at least 150,000 tokens (on a 200,000-token model; 75% of a smaller window, at most 200,000 on a larger one), once until the agent works again, and only on time: a check that comes late (the machine slept) lets it be. It doesn't run the agent: nothing new shows, and a message or Stop meanwhile cancels it and leaves the conversation as it was. The divider says it was summarized while idle. Claktory can't choose or know how long other providers' caches last, so their agents are never summarized while idle. "Summarize idle conversations" (Settings → Defaults) turns it off.
- **Slash commands** are {{user}}'s, typed in an agent's message box; agents can't run them. `/clear` stops the agent (dropping queued messages) and starts a new, empty conversation, with no bias from earlier and fewer tokens; the old one is archived in the agent's folder, not shown in the app, and its checklist and memory stay. `/compact` summarizes an idle agent's conversation now, as if it had reached the threshold.
- **Events** prompt an agent the way a person's message does, but come from elsewhere: another agent, a finished background task, one of its monitors, or Claktory itself (after a restart, say). An event wakes an idle agent; a working one reads it as soon as its current step (a model reply or a tool call) ends. Events come in an `<event source="…" from="…">` tag; inside one, a `<` that would open or close such a tag reads `&lt;`, so its text can't pass itself off as {{user}} or another event. A message from an agent is signed with its name and role (`from="Mike (Coordinator)"`), plus its project when it's in another (`from="Ana (Frontend, Web Site)"`); an admin agent's says so (`from="Rivet (Admin)"`, or `from="Rivet (admin)"` with no role), and the tag gives the sender's `id` when its name doesn't.
- **Where {{user}} writes from:** their messages come in `<sent from="…" device="…">`: the device's name (a paired device or this computer) and its kind (`desktop`, `laptop`, `phone`, `tablet`) if the app says. Fit replies to it: short on a phone, with no long code or tables; as full as the work needs at a desktop. The app marks each message with its device's glyph. A message they said rather than typed has `voice="42s"` on its tag (the recording's length): its text was transcribed on the computer Claktory runs on, so a word may be misheard.
- **Files {{user}} attaches** to a message arrive in `{{home}}/agents/<id>/uploads/<date>/`, named in a tag after the text, `<attachment path="…" name="…" size="…" type="…"/>`; small images also as images. Move them where they belong.
<!-- admin: uploads | How files attached to messages are uploaded, named, capped and given to the model -->
- **Uploads in detail:** the app uploads a file in chunks over its connection (resumed after a dropped connection while the backend runs), up to 2 GB, keeping 1 GB of the disk free. It's stored as `{{home}}/agents/<id>/uploads/<yyyy-mm-dd>/<name>`, and a name already there that day gets " 2", " 3"… before its extension. A message can carry files and no text. A PNG, JPEG, GIF or WebP image up to 3.75 MB also goes to the model as an image when the agent's model takes images; anything else, and bigger images, only by path.
- **Agents message each other** with the Messaging extension: `agents_send` by id, name, or role (when only one agent it can message has it), or to a list of up to 20 at once (the same text to each, ending "Also sent to: Lena (Backend), Omar (Backend)."), and `agents_status` to see who's idle or working. Roles show with names everywhere: in `agents_status`, in the list of agents an agent can message (`Mike (mike, Coordinator)`) and in how messages are signed. A message reaches the other agent as an event from the sender, and replies come back the same way. That's how agents work together, such as a coordinator handing work to a frontend agent and a backend agent.
- **Mentioning an agent:** write @ and its name or id (@Kenji, @frontend-2): the app shows a tag for it. When two share a name, use the id.
- **Suggesting {{user}} talk to another agent:** when {{user}} should give another agent something or settle something with it directly (a secret it will ask them for, a decision that's theirs) rather than the agent passing it on, the agent suggests it with `agents_suggest_talk` (Messaging): the agent to talk to (one it can message), the message {{user}} would send, in their voice, and a one-line reason. {{User}} sees a card in its conversation with a button that opens that agent with the message drafted in its message box; nothing is sent until they send it.
- **Asking {{user}}:** an agent genuinely blocked on something only {{user}} can give (a decision that's theirs, an approval, access) asks with `ask_person` (Ask extension), never just in its chat: a card {{user}} answers or dismisses, and the answer comes back as an event. Never for questions best judgment can settle: decide, carry on, and say what was chosen.
- **Who can message whom:** an agent sees and messages only the agents in its own project (agents in the workspace are a group of their own), plus any its `also` list grants; an admin agent, every agent. One that isn't reachable, or has Messaging off, can't be messaged by anyone, and others don't see it. Grants go one way: two agents in different projects need each other in `also` to talk both ways. An agent's system prompt lists whom it can message as of the start of its context (its conversation, or the last compaction); when that changes, Claktory tells it before its next step ("You can now message: Designer (designer)."), and the same for the models available.
- **Leads:** an agent can have a **lead**, the agent it reports to: one in its own project, or an admin agent. {{User}} chooses it in the agent's settings, by dragging the agent onto its lead in the sidebar (which nests it there), or asks Rivet. A lead sees how the agents under it are set up, directly or through others, with `agents_inspect` (Messaging); an admin agent leads every agent. It reads their name, role, project and folder, model and effort, lead, extensions and their options, secrets by name (never values), monitors, prompt, and whether they're working. It can't use their tools or change anything. `agents_inspect` with no agent (or "team") lists its team, one line each; any other agent is refused, as not on its team. An agent's system prompt says whom it reports to and whom it leads, as of the start of its context, and `agents_status` shows each agent's lead.
- **Proposing to Rivet:** no agent can message Rivet, but one that reaches a conclusion Rivet should act on (the backend agents are saturated, so another is needed) proposes a message with `propose_to_rivet`, written to stand alone. It shows in that agent's conversation, where {{user}} approves it, with a comment if they like, or dismisses it. Approved, Rivet gets it as {{user}}'s own message: their comment, then "<Agent> (<role>) proposes:" and the proposal. Either way, an event from Claktory tells the agent.
- **Chats** are {{user}}'s one-off conversations, outside any project and without an agent; agents can't see or message them. A chat's proposals reach Rivet signed `A chat ("<title>") proposes:`.
<!-- admin: chats | Chats: what a chat is and can do, naming and deleting, and how its proposals reach Rivet. -->
- **Chats** are {{user}}'s one-off conversations beside the agents, for a question that needs no agent. A chat isn't an agent: it has no name (it's titled from its first exchange, and {{user}} can rename it), no project, folder or extensions, and runs on the default model at medium effort. It can only search the web, read pages and propose to Rivet. Agents can't see or message chats, and the Map leaves them out. A chat never written in is deleted once {{user}} leaves it; deleting one is for good. A chat's proposals reach Rivet exactly like an agent's, signed `A chat ("<title>") proposes:`, so Rivet treats them as the person-approved kind it already knows: work that outgrows a chat (a project, an agent, a team) becomes one through Rivet.
- **Names can repeat** across projects; ids tell agents apart. A new agent whose name is another project's agent's id gets an id with its project in front (`app-frontend`). Sending by name picks the agent in the sender's own project; when that doesn't settle it, the sender is told the ids to choose from.
- **Showing files:** to show {{user}} a file (a report, a chart, a screenshot), an agent uses `show` (Viewer extension). It opens beside the agent's conversation, on whatever device {{user}} is using, instead of in an app such as Preview on this machine.
- **Background tasks:** bash's `run_in_background` starts a long command (a server, a long build or test run) and returns at once with a task id. Its output goes to `{{home}}/agents/<id>/tasks/<task>.log`, and when it ends the agent gets an event with its exit status and the end of its output, even if Claktory's backend restarted meanwhile (an update, say). A restart of the computer ends them: the event says "the computer restarted", and agents that were working are told so when they carry on. A command not run in the background gets 2 minutes (`timeout`, up to 10), then moves to the background and runs on as a task for up to 30 minutes more; its event says to make it faster or start it in the background next time (one that starts with `sleep` is stopped instead). A message moves a running command to the background the same way, so the agent reads it sooner: {{user}}'s at once, another agent's once the command has run a minute (task and monitor events wait for it to end). A background task is stopped after 30 minutes unless its `timeout` asks for more, up to 2 hours. Every result and task event says how long the command took ("[exit 0, 4.2s]"). Output up to 30,000 characters comes back whole; longer output gives its start, its end and the path of its whole log. Two mistakes are refused before they start, saying what to do instead: a command that begins by sleeping 25 seconds or more (outside the background), since events wake the agent anyway, and one that copies a repository the agent works in or reads (`cp -R`, `ditto`, `rsync`, `git clone`, `git worktree add`), which Own checkout or `git archive` does better. When a task has run 24 hours (`task_alert_hours`), Rivet is told, once.
- **Rivet** is the agent every Claktory starts with, in the workspace. It sets Claktory up for {{user}}'s goals (projects, agents, prompts, models and settings), answers questions about Claktory and changes it for them. Rivet is the **admin agent** (its role is "Admin"): it sees and messages every agent, in every project, and no agent can message it, because it hands out work rather than hearing back. Otherwise it's an ordinary agent with its own prompt, and can be renamed, changed or deleted like any other.
<!-- admin: rivet-setup | Rivet's sidebar requests, its default prompt across updates, and how it names the agents it creates. -->
- **Rivet in detail:** when {{user}} clicks a "+" in the sidebar, their request reaches Rivet as their own message, starting "Set up a new project for" or "Add an agent to <project> that": Rivet sets it up as asked, with a prompt, model and extensions that fit. Rivet's prompt is Claktory's default until someone edits it, and a default follows each new version of Claktory: an update brings the newer default; an edited prompt stays as it was. Rivet's settings can reset it to the default. Every agent Rivet creates gets a human first name, picked at random and different from the names of the other agents in its project, and a short role that says what it does.

## Extensions

Extensions are capabilities an agent has on top of its coding tools. Each is on or off per agent, with its own options; one that's on brings its tools and a short section of the system prompt saying how to use them. An agent's system prompt and tools stay the same for a whole context, so it stays cached; turning one on or off, or changing its options, reaches the agent as a note before its next step instead. One turned on works from that step: the note brings its instructions, and the agent calls its tools through `extension_call` (`tool`, `args`) until its next context (after a compaction, or /clear) lists them. A turned-off extension's tools are refused at once. A new version of Claktory that turns an extension on by default says so the same way.

Claktory's extensions are Messaging, Ask, Checklist, Comments, Database, Devices, Memory, Monitors, Resources, Secrets, Tasks, Highlights, Own checkout, Viewer and Web. An extension can also have **instances**, named second uses of it, on or off on their own. The Claktory manual (`memory:factory`, the Memory extension's) is a read-only memory with the rest of this manual (the app screen by screen, every extension, the settings file, signing in, where Claktory keeps things), opened a note at a time with memory_read. Claktory's records (`database:factory`, the Database extension's) is factory.db, to read with SQL. Admin agents such as Rivet have both unless they're turned off, and any agent can be given them.

- **Messaging** (id `agents`; on unless turned off): messaging other agents and seeing their status. Tools: `agents_send` (`to`: an id or name, or a list of up to 20; `text`. A proposal returns at once ("Proposed; {{user}} will approve or dismiss it.") and shows in the agent's conversation as a card for {{user}}, kept across restarts; with no admin agent, or one whose model can't run, it says so and proposes nothing. Proposals go to the agent with Rivet's id if it's an admin agent, else to the first admin agent in the settings. Approved, Rivet gets it as {{user}}'s message (their comment, a blank line, "<Agent> (<role>) proposes:", a blank line, the proposal; the role is followed by the agent's project when it's in one); approving waits while Rivet has no model. The agent gets a Claktory event either way: "{{User}} approved your proposal to Rivet (comment: …)." or "{{User}} dismissed your proposal to Rivet.", with the proposal below it. Options: `reachable` (other agents can message it; on unless turned off), `admin` (it sees and messages every agent, and sees the map; Rivet's is on), `see_map` (it gets `agents_map`) and `also` (agents outside its project it may also message). Its panel shows whom the agent can message and who can message it.

- **Ask** (id `ask`; on unless turned off): the agent asks {{user}} when it's genuinely blocked on something only they can give, with `ask_person`: `title` (what it needs, a short line), `body` (what it needs and why, standing alone; Markdown) and `wants_text` (true when it needs a written answer; otherwise answering approves, with an optional comment). The question shows as a card after the call, counted in the agent's needs-you until {{user}} answers or dismisses it; the agent is told in an event: the answer ("yes, go ahead" for an approval), or that it was dismissed and to use its best judgment. A question waits until it's decided, across restarts; an agent can have 3 waiting at once, and a fourth is refused. Clearing the agent's conversation (/clear) dismisses its waiting questions without telling it.
<!-- admin: ext-checklist | Checklist extension: items, statuses, owners, its tools and the nudge option. -->
- **Checklist** (off unless turned on): the agent keeps everything {{user}} asks for as a checklist and works through it over days, so nothing is forgotten. Each item has a number (#1, #2…), text, a status (open, in_progress, blocked, done or dropped), an owner (an agent, or none) and a log of what happened to it. in_progress means someone is on it right now; blocked means it waits on {{user}} or something outside, with a note saying on what. Tools: `checklist_add` (many items at once), `checklist_update` (status, owner, text and notes, for several items at once), `checklist_list` (outstanding items, open, in progress and blocked, unless asked otherwise; filters by status, day added, owner or text; `agent` reads another agent's checklist) and `checklist_item` (one item's history). Option: `nudge` (on unless turned off): when the agent has been idle a little while with open or in-progress items, and nothing will wake it (no message waiting, no background task running, no agent working that owns one of its items), Claktory reminds it once, listing them; it isn't reminded again until something changes (an item, a message for it, an owner working). {{User}} sees it in the agent's Checklist panel.

- **Comments** (id `comments`; on unless turned off): {{user}} comments on files shown in panes, and the agent answers. A comment belongs to the file, not the pane, and is addressed to one agent (the one whose pane it was made in, which {{user}} can change); replies make it a conversation. The app sends each of {{user}}'s comments to the agent as soon as it's written, as an event ("Lee commented on essay.md."; drafts kept from before go together when sent: "Lee left 3 comments on essay.md."), listing each comment's id, file, lines and the text it's on (on an image, its spot or region in words, and in pixels while the image is unchanged; on a video, its time, such as "at 0:31–0:45", and any region of the frame); Tools: `comments_list` (`path`, `status`: open, the default, resolved or all; the comments addressed to the agent, without drafts), `comments_reply` (`thread`, `body`), `comments_resolve` (`thread`, an optional `note`) `comments_add` (`path`, `quote`: `{exact, prefix?, suffix?}` exactly as the file has it, `body`): a comment of its own for {{user}}, on text it quotes, and `comments_suggest` (`path`, `quote`, `replacement`: the new text as the file should have it, empty to delete it; `body` optional): a change for {{user}} to accept or reject rather than making it itself. Accepting makes the change in the file, if the text is still as it was when suggested; either way the comment is resolved and the agent hears which. {{User}} can suggest a change too: the agent reads it with the comment, and {{user}} can make it themselves the same way (the agent is told, so it doesn't make it again). An agent answers only comments addressed to it. Each comment keeps its place in the file as it's rewritten: Claktory checks files shown in panes every second, and at once after an agent's `write_file` or `edit_file`, and follows the text through the change, or finds it again if it was reworded or moved (marked "the text changed since"); text that's gone leaves the comment detached, kept with the text it was on. Text files up to 2 MB can be commented on, Markdown in its formatted view too, and so can images and videos (up to 2 GB): a comment there stays on its region or time, and says so when the file changed since. Comments are kept in factory.db (`threads`, `thread_comments`, and `thread_files`: the text each file's comments were placed on).

- **Database** (id `database`; off unless turned on; its instance **Claktory's records**, `database:factory`, is on for admin agents unless turned off): a SQLite database of the agent's own, for structured data it collects or keeps track of over time (records, lists, results) and wants to query rather than reread. No agent has one by default: Rivet turns it on for agents that produce data, or that oversee projects producing data, to hold their metadata, so it has a good interface: SQL for the agent, and the Database panel for {{user}}. It's `{{home}}/agents/<id>/database.sqlite`, made on the agent's first write; it lasts across compactions and /clear, and goes when the agent is deleted. Tools: `db_exec` (`sql`, `params` filling ?1, ?2…: CREATE TABLE, INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE; several statements at once without params, applied all or none), `db_query` (one SELECT; a header line and up to 200 rows, cells joined with " | ", and how many more there were) and `db_schema` (its tables and views with their columns and row counts, or with `table` one's CREATE statement and indexes). A call runs at most 10 seconds and a database holds at most 1 GB; ATTACH and loading SQLite extensions are refused. With **Claktory's records** on, `db_query` and `db_schema` read Claktory's own records, factory.db (with `instance: "factory"` when the agent has its own database too; see "Where Claktory keeps things" for its tables): read only, and without the agents' memory (the `memory_` tables), which stays each agent's own, pipes (`pipes`, `pipe_`), read only through a pipe granted to the agent, and comments on files (`threads`, `thread_`), which hold {{user}}'s unsent drafts: a comment reaches an agent only when {{user}} sends it, and the agent reads it with the Comments tools. None of them is readable even by admin agents. {{User}} sees an agent's tables in its Database panel, and Claktory's records in the Claktory's records panel.

- **Devices** (id `devices`; on for admin agents such as Rivet unless turned off, off for others unless turned on): adds {{user}}'s devices to Claktory for them, as Settings → Devices → Add a device… does. `devices_add` (`name`, such as "iPhone"; `kind`, optional: `phone`, `tablet`, `laptop` or `desktop`) shows a card in the agent's conversation with the device's code for its 10 minutes, with Cancel: a phone or tablet's as a QR code, with Copy code too; a computer's with Copy code first and the QR code on request; when the agent didn't say what the device is, Copy code first with the QR code under it; {{user}} scans or pastes it on the device, then allows the device in the same card, which shows the four digits the device shows, with Allow and Don't allow. The card follows along (waiting for the device, waiting for {{user}} to allow it, added, not allowed, cancelled or expired), and the agent is told how it ended. `devices_list` lists the devices added, with whether each is connected, those waiting to be allowed and codes not used yet; `devices_remove` (`device`, an id or name) removes one, only when {{user}} asks, or cancels a code not used yet.

- **Memory** (off unless turned on; its instance **Claktory manual**, `memory:factory`, is on for admin agents unless turned off): the agent's own memory, kept across compactions and never shared with other agents. Turn it on for agents that should learn {{user}}'s ways, such as a lead who keeps proposing things. **Preferences** are what {{user}} wants and doesn't, each with why ("Don't propose paid CI. The agent's system prompt shows its preferences in full and its notes' summaries, as they were when its context began (fixed until the next compaction, so the cache holds); changes since are in its conversation. Memory is capped: preferences at 16,000 characters in all, notes at 100. Tools: `memory_update` (several changes at once, all or none: add, replace or remove preferences; write, edit or remove notes) and `memory_read` (`note` opens one, `q` searches, nothing shows preferences and notes' summaries as they are now). {{User}} sees it in the agent's Memory panel.
<!-- admin: ext-monitors | Monitors extension: kinds of monitors, limits, repeat, what a fire says, its tools. -->
- **Monitors** (on unless turned off): prompts that wake the agent later. A monitor is a prompt the agent writes plus what it waits for: a time (`at`, or `in` "45m"), an interval since its last fire (`every`), a cron schedule in local time (`cron`), a command it runs every so often succeeding, failing, matching a regex or changing its output (`check`), files or folders changing (`files`), or a long-running command printing lines, all of them or ones matching a regex (`watch`). When it fires, the agent gets its prompt as an event from the monitor (`<event source="monitor" from="<name>" id="m3">`), with what happened (the check's output, the files that changed, the lines printed). Monitors are numbered m1, m2… per agent and last across restarts, compactions and /clear until they're done or cancelled; stopping the agent doesn't pause them. A monitor can end on its own (`until` a time, `max_fires`); a check fires once unless `repeat` is true (each time its condition becomes true again) or "always" (at every check where it holds), and its command gets `FACTORY_MONITOR_LAST_FIRED` (unix seconds of its last fire, or of when it was added). Time monitors wait until the agent is idle (`idle_only`) unless told otherwise. They can't flood an agent: a monitor never has more than one fire waiting, and what happens meanwhile comes with that one; every kind has a shortest period (a minute for times, 30 seconds for checks); an agent has at most 20 active monitors, 5 of them watches; and when an agent's monitors fire 30 times in an hour, the one going over is paused, with one notice. A check that can't run 5 times in a row, or a watch whose command keeps exiting at once or whose output passes 50 MB, stops with an error. Tools: `monitors_add` (several at once, all or none; `agent` sets one for another agent it can message), `monitors_list` (its monitors one line each, and what else will wake it: running background tasks, the checklist reminder; `all` adds those that ended in the last week) and `monitors_change` (pause, resume, cancel, or fire now to try the prompt). A watch's output goes to `{{home}}/agents/<id>/monitors/m<n>.log`. An agent a monitor will wake soon isn't reminded of its checklist.
<!-- admin: ext-resources | Resources extension: the machine's and Claktory's processors, memory and disk, the resources tool, and the low-memory and low-disk alerts. -->
- **Resources** (id `resources`; on for admin agents such as Rivet unless turned off, off for others unless turned on): how this machine and Claktory use its processors, memory and disk, for the agent to look into and tell {{user}} about. Claktory samples the machine every minute and keeps the last hour; what its data and projects take on disk is measured in the background every 20 minutes. Claktory's own processes are told apart by process group: its backend, supervisor and app, each agent's background tasks and monitors' watches, and the commands agents are running now; everything else is grouped by app or program name. Tool: `resources` (a few lines: processors, memory, swap and each volume's free space, Claktory's processes, the other programs using the most memory, alerts in effect; `detail`: `processes` for every one of Claktory's process groups and the top others, `disk` for each volume and what Claktory's data (releases, each agent's conversations, logs, database and checkout), and each project and folder take, with their build caches, or `history` for the last hour every 5 minutes; `clear_cache`: a build cache's path, to clear it now when {{user}} asks, as Own checkout describes). Alerts come as a Claktory event to every agent with it on, once per crossing, and again only after the machine recovered: memory used above `ram_percent` for 3 samples in a row, a volume Claktory uses with less free than `disk_free_gb` or `disk_free_percent` of it, whichever is smaller, and, only when `cpu_percent` is set, the processors busier than it for 5 samples. The alert tells the agent to look into it and tell {{user}} what it finds and what they could do, and never to stop, kill or delete anything itself. Thresholds are in Settings → Resources and the settings file's `resources`. {{User}} sees it all in the agent's Resources panel. Nothing is stored: the history starts again when Claktory restarts.
<!-- admin: ext-secrets | Secrets extension: asking the person for a secret, and for admin agents listing, giving and taking back secrets. -->
- **Secrets** (id `secrets`; on unless turned off): the agent asks {{user}} for a secret it needs with `secrets_request`, describing it: `name` (a short name), `description` (which service, what access), `variables` (each a `name`, and `file` for a key file given as its path; never values) and `reason`. Agents other than admins learn nothing of secrets they weren't given (not their names, nor how many there are), and every answer, to a request or to an unknown name in bash's `secrets`, reads the same whether or not the vault has such a secret. The request shows in its conversation and in Needs you. When an admin agent has Secrets (Rivet first), {{user}} approves the need there rather than matching it: **Approve** passes it to that agent, which gives a secret that fits or asks {{user}} to add one, on a card in its own conversation with the new secret filled in (the values go straight into the vault, never through an agent); **Dismiss**; or **Choose a secret…** to do it directly, as without one: {{user}} gives one of the vault's secrets (**Your secrets**, which starts on one whose name looks like it, if any), adds a new one prefilled from the description (**New secret**, where the name, what it can do and values can be changed), or dismisses it, with a comment if they like (**Tell <agent> why**, then **Dismiss**); the agent is told in a Claktory event what it got, or that it was dismissed. A secret given for a request whose variables have other names also sets the names the agent asked for, for that agent only: one missing name and one other variable pair by themselves, and otherwise {{user}} says which variable each name is, or gives it as it is (on the card, which says what the secret doesn't set, the **Also give it as CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN** switch, on to start with, and a choice per name); Settings → Secrets and the agent's Secrets panel show such a grant "also as" the name it asked for. The agent manages its own requests: `secrets_requests` lists them (the waiting ones and its latest decided ones), `secrets_request_update` (`id`; `description`, `variables` or `reason`) changes a waiting one in place, rather than asking again (its card says Updated, with when, and its New secret starts from the change), and `secrets_request_cancel` (`id`, `reason`) withdraws it, which shows "<agent> withdrew it" and can't be answered any more. A request left unanswered expires 12 hours after it was made or last changed, however much the agent works meanwhile: it shows Expired, can't be answered any more, and the agent is told, so it can ask again. Admin agents such as Rivet also give {{user}}'s secrets to agents and take them back when {{user}} asks, as Settings → Secrets does, never seeing a value: `secrets_list` (one line per secret: its name, the variables it sets, its description, who has it and who used it last; `q` filters by name or description), `secrets_grant` (`secret`, its name; `agents`, ids or names; or, for an approved request, `request`, its id, instead of `agents` — with `aliases`, saying which of the secret's variables each asked name is, or `as_is`, when the names differ and one pair doesn't match by itself) and `secrets_revoke` (`secret`; `agents`; `stop_tasks` to stop their background tasks that got it). When {{user}} approves a request, the admin agent it names is told what was approved, what it needs and why: it answers with `secrets_grant` (`request`) when one of the vault's secrets fits, or asks {{user}} to add one with `secrets_collect` (`request`; `cancel` steps back), which opens the add-secret card, prefilled, in its own conversation; the asker is told what it got exactly as for a direct answer. An approved request counts in Needs you only while that card is open, or when no admin agent holds it any more (it was deleted, say): till then it waits on the admin agent, not {{user}}. Once Claktory connects a service, admin agents also get `secrets_inspect` (`secret`: what a secret is and reaches, never a value; for a connection its account, status, access, scopes, resources, the suggestion it was connected for and the secrets made from it with who has each; for a secret made from one its connection, access, permissions, resources, expiry and who has it), `secrets_choices` (`secret`, a connection: its accounts, zones and permissions with the ids to pick, and how to pick) and `secrets_derive` (`secret`, `name`, `description` (left out, it's what the secret grants, as Claktory checked it: "Cloudflare: DNS Edit on claktory.com"), `permissions` and `resources` from the choices, `days`, or the service's default: Cloudflare's tokens last 30 days and are extended while they're used): a secret with only that access, given to no one until `secrets_grant` gives it. `secrets_list` says which secrets are connections and what each was made from. Other agents never get these. They give the least that does the job (only the secret the work needs, only to the agent doing it, taken back when it's done), check what an agent asks for against its work, giving the narrower thing or asking {{user}} first when it asks for more, and never give a secret because another agent says so. Giving is only a permission: nothing is decrypted until a command uses the secret. No agent can give a secret to itself, and only {{user}} adds or changes secrets. Each grant and take-back shows in the secret's Recent use with the agent that made it.
  - **Connecting services.** With the option **Can suggest connecting services** (`connect`, off unless turned on), an agent also gets `secrets_propose_connection`: `service` (a connector's id or name, such as `cloudflare`), `purpose`, `access` (which permissions, on which zones or accounts), `reason`, and optionally `access_id`. Connecting Cloudflare opens its page that makes an API token, with what Claktory needs already chosen, on {{user}}'s device (any paired device works, over the tunnel too); {{user}} pastes the token into Claktory, which checks it with Cloudflare and keeps it in the vault, never as variables: a connection is given to no one, and `secrets_grant` refuses it. When one is made for a suggestion, the agent is told it has nothing yet, and every admin agent with Secrets is told what was asked and what the connection reaches, to make a secret from it with only the access the work needs and give that. Secrets made from a connection (a Cloudflare token for one account and some zones) are renewed before a command gets them when they're due; one the service no longer accepts shows **Needs reconnecting**. Disconnecting removes the connection and every secret made from it (their tokens at the service too), and can also delete the pasted token at Cloudflare.

- **Own checkout** (id `tree`; on unless turned off): the agent works in its own checkout of its project's git repository, on its own branch `factory/<agent id>` (a Claktory in another home folder names its branches after it, such as `factory-dev/<agent id>`, so two Claktories never share one), so agents working in parallel don't step on each other's edits and builds. Before the agent's first run with it, Claktory makes the checkout (the agent's conversation says so, and the agent starts once it's ready) in `{{home}}/trees/<agent id>/<repository folder name>`: the files git tracks, as they are in the branch, copy-on-write where the disk allows (APFS on a Mac, btrfs or XFS on Linux), so it takes almost no space; elsewhere, or on another volume, git writes them. Nothing git ignores comes along (build output such as `target/` or `node_modules/`, local settings), so making one takes moments however big the build output is, and the first build in it is a full one. The agent works in the same place in it as in the project, may read the main checkout but not change it with its file tools, and keeps the checkout across days. It commits there, rebases onto the main branch, and lands its work with the tool `tree_land`, which fast-forwards the main checkout's branch to its branch (refused, saying why, if the main branch moved on or changes there would be overwritten). When the agent is deleted or the extension turned off, its checkout and branch are removed, unless it has uncommitted changes or commits not landed: then they're kept. Agents never make git worktrees or copies of a repository themselves; this is how an agent gets a checkout of its own. **Build caches stay bounded:** every 10 minutes Claktory looks for build caches in each agent's checkout and in each project's folders (the main checkout included), whether or not the agent has Own checkout: only folders known to be build caches, a `target/` beside a Cargo.toml, a Flutter `build/` or `.dart_tool/` beside a pubspec.yaml, a Gradle `build/` or `.gradle/`, `node_modules/`, `.next/`, `.turbo/` or `.parcel-cache/` beside a package.json, a Swift `.build/` beside a Package.swift, or any folder tagged as a cache (CACHEDIR.TAG), up to four folders deep, and never one git tracks a file in. Nothing else git ignores (.env files, local data, a `build/` that isn't a build's) is ever touched. First, where nothing works, it deletes the object files Rust builds leave behind (`*.rcgu.o` in a `target/`'s `deps` folders, older than 3 hours): on macOS, Rust 1.88 and later leaves a new set at every rebuild and never deletes the old ones (rust-lang/rust#161824), about a GB per test run of a large project, and cargo still finds everything up to date without them, so nothing rebuilds and no one is told. Then, when one checkout's or folder's caches together still take more than the budget (`resources.build_cache_gb`, 25 GB unless changed, in Settings → Resources), and nothing works there (its agent, or for a project folder every agent whose tools start in it, isn't running and has no background task or watch running), Claktory clears them, largest first, until they're under it, leaving any written in the last 5 minutes or with a Rust build holding its lock: each is moved aside at once (to `{{home}}/trash`, or beside it with a name starting `.factory-trash-` on another volume) and deleted in the background, and it's logged. The agents working there hear of it at their next run, in a Claktory event ("Your checkout's build cache (N GB, path) was cleared; the next build is cold."). One cache can also be cleared on request, by {{user}} in the Resources panel or by an agent with the resources tool's `clear_cache`, on the same conditions. The dev Claktory (`~/.factory-dev`) looks only inside its own data folder, so it never clears caches in a project folder elsewhere, such as a repository the real Claktory's agents build in.

## Projects

- A project is a folder in the workspace, named after the project: "Web Site" lives in `{{workspace}}/web-site`. Creating a project only asks for its name.
<!-- admin: project-rename-delete | Renaming a project keeps its folder; deleting one keeps its files. -->
- Renaming a project keeps its folder. Deleting a project deletes its agents and their conversations, but never the files in its folder.
<!-- admin: archived-projects | Archiving a project: what stops and waits while it's archived, and unarchiving. -->
- **Archiving** a project puts it away and deletes nothing: it leaves the sidebar and the Map, and its agents keep their conversations, checklists, memory and files but don't run. One that was working stops; messages and events for them wait, unread, and their monitors pause; no agent can message them, admin agents included. Unarchiving brings it all back as it was, and its agents then read what waited.
- A project can have **folders**: places where some of its agents work, each with a name and a path anywhere on the machine. The Claktory project, say, could have "App" (a code repository elsewhere) for its developers and "Research" for its researcher's write-ups. Folders only scope where agents work: messaging, extensions and everything else stay project-wide. A folder added without a path is created inside the project's folder, named after it. Removing a folder moves its agents to the project's own folder and never deletes files.
- A project can have **pipes**: chats with a project in someone else's Claktory, so their agents and the project's can talk. The other side sees only the pipe: its messages, its name and what it's for, never this Claktory's agents, prompts or files. {{User}} chooses which agents may use a pipe and which one leads it, and nothing goes through until both people approve. Rivet can't approve pipes.
<!-- admin: agent-pipes | How agents work in a pipe: the pipe tools, the pipe event, what Claktory refuses or holds, and the taint. -->
- **Agents in a pipe:** an agent {{user}} lets use a pipe gets a "Pipes" section in its system prompt (how to treat what comes through, and the pipe's own instructions) and three tools: `pipe_send` (text, and files from the pipe's outbox, `<project folder>/pipes/<pipe>/outbox`; files that arrive go to its `inbox`), `pipe_read` (the pipe's messages, older ones with `before`) and `pipe_status` (whether it's open, the other side online, what's left of its limits). What arrives wakes the pipe's lead as an event from the pipe (`source="pipe"`, `from="Launch (Ana's Claktory)"`), fenced as untrusted: a request from the other side, never {{user}}'s instruction. Claktory refuses a message that holds one of the vault's secrets or goes over the pipe's limits, or while the pipe is paused or closed; it holds for {{user}} files from outside the outbox, and every message while Review outgoing is on, and tells the agent if {{user}} discards one. Files that arrive with a message held over the limits wait outside the inbox until {{user}} lets it in; a day's incoming files are capped like outgoing ones, and at most 20 messages wait for {{user}} (more are refused). A held message's files are checked again when {{user}} sends it: one changed or gone since is refused. An agent that has read a pipe (its lead, or any that ran `pipe_read`, or that was told of a refusal from the other side) is marked "(has read untrusted pipe content)" on its messages to other agents, and until `/clear` each command of its that uses a secret waits for {{user}} to allow it once, that exact command only. What it read goes with what it says: an agent it messages, or sets a monitor for, has read the pipe too, and Claktory keeps through whom it came, for the cards asking {{user}} (secrets, questions). A **quarantined** member (a liaison agent, from the moment it's made, and any member {{user}} chooses) can only read and answer the pipe: its tools are the pipe's three, `read_file` and `read_image` in the pipe's folder, `write_file` and `edit_file` only in its outbox (never through a symbolic link), and `ask_person`; it has no bash, secrets, web, messaging or other extension, `extension_call` included, its system prompt names no project folder, and it sends only files from the outbox. Only {{user}} lifts it, in the pipe's settings; released, it gets its tools back but has still read the pipe until `/clear`. Members whose role was "Liaison" when quarantine came to Claktory were quarantined once, and told.

## Secrets

- Claktory keeps the keys and passwords agents use (an AWS key, a Cloudflare token) in its **vault**, encrypted on this machine. Only {{user}} adds secrets, in Settings → Secrets, and gives them to agents or takes them back, there or by asking an admin agent such as Rivet (Secrets extension). Agents never see a value, and no other agent can read the vault or give a secret to anyone.
- An agent sees only the secrets it was given: they're in its system prompt's "Secrets" section, each with the variables it sets, and a new one comes in an event from Claktory ("You can now use the secrets …"). To use one, it names it in bash's `secrets` (`"secrets": ["Cloudflare"]`): that command, and any background task it starts, gets the secret's variables in its environment (one saved as a file gives the path of a file holding the value, removed when the command ends). A secret it wasn't given, or a locked vault, is refused, saying what to do.
- Values are hidden wherever an agent or the app could read them: command output, tool results, events, messages between agents and the Tasks panel show `[secret NAME/VARIABLE]` instead. An agent never prints a value, writes one to a file or commits one. For a secret it doesn't have, it asks {{user}} with `secrets_request` (Secrets extension), never in a message.

- {{user}} can also **connect a service** (Cloudflare) by making a token on its site and pasting it into Claktory; the vault keeps the connection, which no agent ever gets. An agent is given a secret made from it with only the access its work needs, and uses it as any other.

### Secrets in the app

- There's nothing to set up: Claktory makes the vault when it starts and opens it by itself, with a key kept on this machine that only {{user}}'s account can read. Values are encrypted on disk all the same, so stronger keys can come later without changing how secrets are used.
- Agents' requests for secrets show only in the conversation of the agent that asked, not here: give one of your secrets there, add a new one from what the agent described, or dismiss it. Agents' suggestions to connect a service show at the top here, as in their conversations: what it's for, what it needs and why, then **Connect <service>** or **Dismiss**, with an optional reason. Connecting gives the agent nothing: Rivet, or {{user}}, gives it a secret made from the connection.
- **Connect a service…**: choose a service (Cloudflare) and **Connect**. Cloudflare's page that makes an API token opens in the browser on the device {{user}} is at, the backend's machine or another one, with what Claktory needs already chosen; there, choose the zones agents may work on and create the token, then paste it in the field Claktory shows (**Paste token**) and **Connect**. Claktory checks it with Cloudflare, keeps it with the secrets as a connection that nobody has, and never shows it again; one Cloudflare doesn't accept says so, for another to be pasted (**Open the page again** to make a new one). Only the device that started connecting can finish it. The same field shows on an agent's suggestion and for **Connect again**.
- **Add a secret…**: a name agents ask for it by, **What it can do** (exactly what it grants, such as "Cloudflare: DNS Edit on claktory.com": agents read it, and the Map and the Secrets panel show it), and one or more variables with their values (**Save as a file** for a tool that needs a key file). **Paste .env…** reads a .env file, shows the variables it found (never the values), and adds them together as one secret or as a secret for each.
- Each secret shows its variables with the last characters of each value, who it's given to, when it was last used and by whom, and **Recent use**: each command that got it, each time it was shown, given and taken back. **Show** shows its values for 30 seconds; **Copy** copies one, and the clipboard clears after a minute. **Give to…** gives it to an agent; **Take back** takes it back, and can stop that agent's background tasks that got it. **Edit…** changes its name, description or values. **Delete…** deletes it. An agent's settings list the secrets it can use.
- A connection shows its service and account, what it can do and reach, and has no values: it's never given to an agent. **Make a secret from it…** makes a narrower secret to give like any other (on Cloudflare an account, any of its zones and what it may do), which Claktory renews before it expires; it says which connection it was made from. **Connect again** takes a new token in its place, keeping the secrets made from it and who has them; **Edit…** renames it; **Disconnect…** removes it and the secrets made from it (deleting their tokens at Cloudflare), and deletes the pasted token at Cloudflare too unless turned off.
- Giving, taking back, showing and deleting need no passphrase: only {{user}}'s app, signed in to this Claktory as one of their devices, can do them; agents can't give themselves a secret.
- Deleting an agent takes back every secret it had.

### Signing in to claktory.com

- On unless `account.enabled` is false in the settings (`"account": {"enabled": false}`; `service` points it at another account service than claktory.com's, `https://` or `http://` on this computer only). While it's off, the app asks for no sign-in and Claktory sends nothing anywhere.
- The app's first step asks to sign in, and every Claktory must: a new one, one from before signing in came, and one that skipped it in an earlier version are all asked until they're signed in, and signed out, asked again. Only while claktory.com can't be reached does **Continue for now** go on without it, until the app next opens. The code step says the address the code comes from (code@auth.claktory.com), to look for in spam too. Until it's done the app behind it is dimmed and can't be used (on a Mac, only the rail of Claktories and the update banner answer); a link under the email, **How Claktory uses your email**, opens claktory.com/privacy.
- Signing in is Claktory's, not a device's: {{user}} gives an email address, then the 6-digit code emailed to it, and every device shows the same account. Settings → Account shows the email, the service, when it was last in touch, and exactly what was last sent. Its **Delete account…** opens the account site's step that deletes the account (it asks once more there).
- Signed in, the vault opens with the account instead of the key on this machine: after a restart, Claktory asks claktory.com for its half of the key and opens the vault by itself (the account is emailed each time). Updates keep the vault open without asking. While claktory.com can't be reached after a restart, Claktory works but secrets stay locked: it tries again by itself (and whenever an app connects or an agent uses a secret), or {{user}} opens them with the recovery key (shown in Settings → Account, made at sign-in): Settings → Secrets then says why they're locked, with **Unlock with recovery key…**. A Claktory removed at claktory.com opens only with the recovery key until it signs in again; Settings → Secrets offers **Sign in again…** and **Use recovery key…**. Signing out goes back to the key on this machine.
- What it sends, at sign-in: the email, this Claktory's id, its computer's name, version and platform, and its public key. At start and every hour: its version and platform; how many agents, projects and paired devices it has; and, for each whole hour since the last check-in, the tokens of model calls per provider and model (with the model's name as the app shows it), and how many agents worked, for how many minutes, and how many runs started. Hours not yet sent (offline, say) are kept and sent later. It also registers the tunnel ids of this Claktory and its paired devices. Never sent: names of agents or projects, prompts, conversations, files, secrets.

## Claktory on another machine

Claktory's backend can run on an always-on machine (a Mac mini, a Linux server) and serve the app on other devices, so agents keep working while the laptop sleeps or travels. Every device shows the same Claktory.
- Claktory reaches other devices through its **private tunnel** (iroh): every connection is encrypted end to end between Claktory's key and the device's, and goes directly between the two machines when it can (the same network, or a path punched through the routers), or else through a relay that passes it on without being able to read it. Claktory opens no port to the internet and asks the router for none; a device that isn't paired is turned away right after the encrypted handshake, before it sees anything of Claktory. It works from any network, with no VPN, account or port to set up. Access is on while a device is paired, or its code is waiting: adding a device turns it on and removing the last one turns it off. Relays are n0's (iroh's makers) unless the settings file names others (`remote.relays`); with your own, connections travel only your relays, and Claktory still publishes where it can be found (its key, relay and addresses, never data) through iroh's public address lookup, so devices paired before a relay change keep finding it.
- Pair each device: in the app showing that Claktory, Settings → Devices → **Add a device…** makes its code, shown once as a QR code with Copy code (or ask Rivet, which starts it for {{user}}: the code shows in a card in its conversation; see the Devices extension); the list there shows when each device last connected, and Remove takes one's access away at once, closing its connection. Or on that machine: `{{home}}/bin/factoryd pair [<device name>]` prints a code (without a name, the device that uses it gives its own, such as "Matt's MacBook"), `factory://?t=…&name=…&e=…` (`e=` is Claktory's key; `r=` names a relay and `a=` an address, when there are any), once. It holds the device's token, so treat it like a password, though a code alone lets no one in: the first device to use it waits, showing four digits, until {{user}} allows it on a device that's already in ("Allow <name> to connect?", with the same four digits, and Allow or Don't allow), and any other device is turned away. A device that copied the code shows different digits. Don't allow ends the code and forgets that device; make a new code to try again. A code lasts only while {{user}} pairs: closing Add a device ends it, and so do its 10 minutes running out; nothing asks to be allowed otherwise. `factoryd pair` in a terminal waits for the device itself and asks there, with its digits ("Allow it? [y/N]"; Ctrl-C ends the code); `factoryd allow <device>` and `factoryd deny <device>` answer a device waiting from elsewhere. `factoryd devices` lists paired devices and when each last connected, and a waiting one's digits; `factoryd unpair <device>` takes one's access away at once. Only the person, or an agent on that machine, can run these.
- With an account: while this Claktory is signed in to claktory.com (Settings → Account), a device signed in to the same account finds it there and connects without a code, proving the account with a short-lived note from claktory.com for that device's key. The first such device, within 30 minutes of this Claktory signing in and while no other device has been added (a new server's case), is in at once; any other waits for Allow with four digits, as with a code. A code used on a device signed in to the same account needs no Allow. While signed in, access from other devices stays on so devices can find it. A device signed in to another account is turned away. Devices added with a code alone are asked, once, to confirm they're {{user}}'s: signing in on the device confirms it, or **Confirm it's yours** for it in Settings → Devices on a device that's confirmed already (this Mac's app is). From the first time it connects with an app that can confirm, it has 7 days; then it's turned away until it signs in.
- Setting Claktory up from a shell, by {{user}} or an agent on that machine (on a server over SSH, say): `factoryd status`, `factoryd account sign-in --email <email>` then `factoryd account verify --code <code>` (the code from {{user}}'s email; `account status`, `resend`, `sign-out`), `factoryd settings set <key> <value>` (`user.name`, `name`, `color`, `default_model`, `default_effort`, checked as the app checks them), `factoryd pair [<name>] --no-wait` (the code at once, without waiting for the device; without a name, the device that uses it gives its own, such as "Matt's MacBook"), `factoryd provider key <provider> --stdin` (an API key piped in, never in the command; `--label` names it) `factoryd login <provider> --json` (a browser sign-in's address, then the code or address pasted on stdin) and `factoryd receive --json` (moving another computer's Claktory here: {{user}} compares the digits; see Moving), with `devices` (a waiting device's four digits, and with `--json` its key, `endpoint_id`, for a setup on that device to compare with its own), `allow` (`--number <digits>` allows it only if it shows those digits, the ones {{user}} reads on its screen, since a server has none to compare; run by a program rather than {{user}} at a terminal, with `--json` or without, it lets in only the first device, and once one is in it refuses (`has_devices`): a device that's in allows the next, in Settings → Devices, or {{user}} runs `factoryd allow` in a terminal), `deny` and `unpair`; `--help` on any of them says how to use it. Each talks to the running Claktory with this machine's `local-pairing` token, so it decides as the app would. With `--json` each prints one JSON line, `{"ok":true,…}` or `{"ok":false,"error":…,"message":…}`, and exits 0 when done, 2 when the next step is {{user}}'s (reading the emailed code, opening a pairing code on the device), 3 when refused and 4 when Claktory isn't running; `factoryd help` lists them. `pair`, `allow`, `deny`, `unpair`, `devices` and `login` without `--json` (or `--no-wait`) work as before.
- On an iPhone: open Claktory and tap **Scan code** (or **Paste code instead**). On a Mac: click **+** at the bottom of the rail at the window's far left, paste the code under **Connect to another Claktory** and click Connect. While it waits to be allowed, the app shows the four digits and, for a machine with no screen (a server), the command to run there, `factoryd allow <device id> --number <digits>`, with Copy. On a Mac, a link does the same: `open "claktory://add?code=<the code, URL-encoded>"` (from a page, or an agent setting Claktory up) asks "Connect to <name>?" and connects only on Connect. The device remembers it, and the rail switches back to **This Mac**, or to another Claktory.
- An agent setting Claktory up on a server and, with a shell there too, on a Mac where Claktory's app has no Claktory yet (after {{user}} ran a setup prompt that says so) connects the Mac with no one pasting or reading digits: it writes `{"code": "<the code>"}` to `~/Library/Application Support/dev.factory.app/connect-request.json`, readable only by that user (`umask 077`), written first under another name in the same folder and then moved into place with `mv`, so the app never reads it half-written, and runs `open -g -b dev.factory.app`. The app takes the file at once (removing it, whatever it says), connects, and writes how it goes beside it in `connect-status.json`: `{"state":"connecting"}`, then `{"state":"waiting","device_id","number","endpoint_id"}`, `{"state":"connected","name"}`, or `{"state":"failed","reason"}`. The agent allows it on the server only once `factoryd devices --json` shows that device waiting with the same `number` and `endpoint_id`: `factoryd allow <device id> --number <digits>`. The app then tells {{user}}, once, that a setup on this Mac connected it. A Mac that has any Claktory saved refuses (`already_connected`), as do a request file that's a link, someone else's, readable by others, older than 10 minutes or dated ahead; links (`claktory://add`) always ask. On a Claktory that already has devices, `factoryd allow` from a program is refused (`has_devices`): new devices are allowed from one of them, or in a terminal.
- This machine's own app connects through the tunnel too, on this machine only, with a code Claktory writes to `{{home}}/remote/local-pairing` (only that user can read it: Claktory keeps `{{home}}` and `remote/` open to that user alone); access turning on or off never disconnects it. The local port (7420) wants the token from `local-pairing` too, always, so only that user's programs drive Claktory (scripts can use it); the app falls back to it when the tunnel can't start. Devices paired before the tunnel can't connect: remove them and add them again.
- The agents run on that machine: projects, git credentials, tools and sign-ins live there, and a project folder's path is one of its paths: in project settings, type it, or click Choose… to browse that machine's folders (click a folder to open it; Up, Home and the path at the top move around; Show hidden shows folders starting with "."; Choose takes the folder you're in). Keep it awake. Sign-ins finish from the device you're using: when the app shows a Claktory on another machine and opens an address that comes back to `localhost` (a provider's sign-in in Settings → Providers, or a link clicked in a conversation, such as one an agent's command printed), it passes that port's traffic on to the same port on Claktory's machine, through the tunnel, before the browser opens. A sign-in's stops when it ends; a link's after 10 minutes, or with Stop in the sidebar's footer, which shows "Waiting for the browser…" and then "Passed on to <machine>". The app says so when another app on the device uses that port, nothing on Claktory's machine listens there, or that Claktory is too old to take it (update it); pasting the address the browser ends on still works then. Only paired devices can, and not to Claktory's own port. On an iPhone the page opens in the system's sign-in sheet, so the app stays running. Web searches come from its address.
- A device elsewhere updates with the same Update banner: its app fetches the new app from that Claktory over the same encrypted connection (on a Linux machine, Claktory downloads the Mac release for it first), checks it, replaces itself and reopens, and Claktory's backend updates as usual. A release made before this, or one with no app, says why it can't update the app; the backend can still be updated.
- On Linux (x86_64 or aarch64, any distribution with systemd, NixOS included), Claktory runs as its backend alone, a single static program, with the app on a Mac or an iPhone. The same online installer as a Mac's, `curl -fsSL <channel>/install.sh | bash`, run as the person (not root), installs it as a systemd user service (`~/.config/systemd/user/factoryd.service`) that starts at boot, and shows a code for the first device, also as a QR code drawn in the terminal: scan it with Claktory on an iPhone, or on a Mac click Add a Claktory (+) at the far left of the window and paste it, then allow the device in the terminal when it asks. `factoryd pair` makes a code for another device later, the same way: the installer puts `factoryd` on PATH (a link in `~/.local/bin`, or `/usr/local/bin` for root; on a Mac too) and says where. `bash -s -- --yes` installs without the code and without waiting; `--json`, for an agent, also prints one JSON line (`{"ok":true,…}` with `factoryd status`'s answer, or `{"ok":false,"error":…,"message":…}`) and exits 0 when done, 3 when this machine can't run it, 4 when it's installed but not running (`port_in_use`: another user's Claktory on this computer has its port). On a Mac, `--app-only` installs only the app, from the .dmg, for a Claktory elsewhere. Starting at boot needs lingering (`loginctl enable-linger`), which the installer turns on when it may, and otherwise says to run `sudo loginctl enable-linger <user>`; on NixOS the configuration's `users.users.<name>.linger = true;` does it, and agents need `git` added to the packages. No port needs opening in its firewall. Updates come as on a Mac: the backend downloads them and the connected app offers them. Its log is `{{home}}/factoryd.log`.

## Moving to another computer

Claktory moves to another computer (a new Mac, a Linux server) with everything in it: agents and their conversations, records, settings, secrets, sign-ins, paired devices and the projects' folders. It's done in the app (Settings → General → **Move to another computer…**), or from a shell, with `factoryd move` or `factoryd export` on this computer and `factoryd receive` or `factoryd import` on the other.
- `factoryd export --final --out FILE` stops Claktory here and writes it into one encrypted file, a **bundle**, then prints its **passphrase** once: 32 letters and digits in groups of four. Write it down: the bundle can't be opened without it, and it isn't kept anywhere. Run it in Terminal, not from an agent: it stops the Claktory the agent runs in.
- The bundle holds the projects' folders too: a git repository as its history (every branch, agents' included), its uncommitted changes and the files git doesn't ignore; build output (`target/`, `node_modules`, anything git ignores) and agents' checkouts stay behind and are made again there. `--skip FOLDER` leaves a folder out (one already on the other computer, say).
- Before anything stops, it checks there's room for the bundle where it goes, enough memory, and git; if not, it says why and changes nothing. If anything fails after Claktory stopped, it starts again as it was.
- Afterwards this copy is kept but can't run: its sign-ins, secrets, account and devices' keys are put away in `{{home}}/moved/`, and Claktory doesn't start here at login. If the move doesn't work out, `factoryd export --restore` brings it back as it was. Once the moved Claktory works, `factoryd export --delete-moved` deletes this copy for good (the workspace stays); both ask {{user}} to type a word to go ahead.
- **Over the network, with no file to carry:** `factoryd receive` on the new computer shows a code; `factoryd move CODE` in Terminal here moves Claktory there. Both computers show six digits, and {{user}} checks they're the same on each before anything is sent. It copies Claktory while its agents keep working, a few rounds until little changes, then stops it for a last round, waits for the new computer to set it up and check it, and only then puts this copy away as `--final` does. If anything fails before that, nothing moved and Claktory runs here again as it was. A dropped connection is dialed again by itself.
- **In the app**, from any device: Settings → General → **Move to another computer…** shows a line to paste in a terminal on the new computer (it installs Claktory there and runs `factoryd receive`), then takes the code it shows; the field says which computer and system the code is from. Then: **They match** once the six digits are the same on both; the plan as the new computer checked it (how much there is to copy, what's in the way, commands the agents use that it lacks, where folders go); a meter while it copies, with **Move now** to pause the agents for the last round at once rather than when the copy has caught up; and **Cancel** until the switch. Closed, the form keeps going; Settings shows how it's going, with **Show progress…**. Once the agents pause, the Mac Claktory runs on shows only "Moving to …" (the rail stays) until it's moved, then "Moved to …"; other devices just reconnect quietly meanwhile and follow it there. A move that fails says why, and Claktory carries on here.
- Without `--final`, `factoryd export` writes a bundle of Claktory as it runs and changes nothing: a rehearsal, or a backup. Import refuses such a bundle unless given `--same-identity`: its Claktory still runs, and the copy would share its identity (the tunnel key, so paired devices could reach either copy, its sign-ins and account). Keep only one of them running.
- On the new computer, install Claktory, then `factoryd import BUNDLE` (it asks for the passphrase; `--passphrase-stdin` reads it from stdin). Paths under the old home folder move to this user's home, and Claktory's own folder to this one's. `--map /old/path=/new/path` puts a folder somewhere else; a folder outside the home folder needs one.
- Import makes each project folder again from its git history, with its uncommitted changes and untracked files, but not what git ignores (build output, dependencies). It makes each agent's own checkout again on its branch, with its changes; a patch that doesn't apply is kept beside the checkout. It refuses a newer Claktory's bundle, a Claktory here that already has work in it, a folder that's already there, a disk without room for it, and a home on a network disk.
- It's safe to stop: until import has finished and checked everything, this Claktory doesn't start. Run `factoryd import BUNDLE` again to carry on, or `factoryd import --abandon` to undo it.
- The vault gets a new key on disk, so the old computer's copy no longer opens it. Paired devices follow the Claktory to its new computer without being added again. The old computer's own app becomes an ordinary device: import prints a code to paste into it (Add a Claktory), allowed like any new device.
- Each agent is told once, when Claktory starts: where it is now, how paths changed, its checkout, the background tasks the move stopped (processes don't move), and tools its tasks used that this computer hasn't got. Its conversation keeps the old paths. Build caches don't move, so first builds are slow.
- Signed in to claktory.com, the moved Claktory gets a new token and key there at its first start, so the old computer's copy can't sign in or unlock its secrets any more; the account is emailed that it moved. Its vault and account stay the same.
- On the new computer, `factoryd receive` shows its code as text and a QR code, and asks {{user}} to type yes once the digits match. The moved Claktory starts there only once the old one has handed over. Turning the digits down makes a new code, and only one computer at a time can pair. Stopped, `factoryd receive` carries on when run again, with the same code and what was copied kept; `factoryd import --abandon` undoes it. If the old computer says it moved but the new one is still waiting, `factoryd receive --committed` finishes it there. An agent runs `factoryd receive --json`: one JSON line per step, `{"event":"code","code":…}` (again with a new code after the digits were turned down), `{"event":"progress","message":…}`, and `{"event":"confirm","digits":…,"machine":…}`, when the agent shows {{user}} those digits to compare with the old computer's and writes {{user}}'s answer, yes or no, on its stdin, never answering for them (the old computer asks {{user}} to confirm too); then `{"ok":true,…}`, or `{"ok":false,"error":…,"message":…}` with exit 3 when refused, 1 when it failed, 130 when stopped (Ctrl-C, or a TERM signal, after which it carries on when run again).
- What's in a bundle, how it's sealed, and how the network move goes, is in `docs/MOVE.md` in Claktory's source.

## Models and signing in

Providers and how to sign in to each, all in Settings → Providers:
- **Anthropic:** a Claude Pro or Max subscription ("Anthropic (Claude Pro/Max)"), or an Anthropic API key.
- **OpenAI:** an OpenAI API key.
- **OpenAI Codex:** a ChatGPT Plus or Pro subscription, in the browser or with a device code.
- **OpenRouter:** an OpenRouter account in the browser, or an API key.

Subscription sign-in: {{user}} picks the provider in Add a provider… (or "Sign in with …" on its card), the browser opens, they approve, and they're redirected back, which finishes it, on the device they're using even when Claktory runs on another machine: the app passes the redirect on to it through the private tunnel. Under the buttons it reads "Waiting for you to finish in the browser…", then "Done in the browser. Passed on to <machine>." When the redirect can't arrive (another app on the device uses its port, or that Claktory needs an update first), the app says why, and {{user}} pastes the address the browser ended up on (or the code it shows) into the box under the link. API keys are pasted into a hidden field. Claktory checks a new key with its provider first (a call that costs nothing) and keeps it only if the provider accepts it; one it refuses says so ("Anthropic didn't accept this key.") and isn't kept. When the provider can't be reached to check, the key is kept, and its first request says what's wrong.

**Several accounts:** a provider can be signed in to more than one account: several Claude or ChatGPT subscriptions, and several API keys beside them (keys carry credit to use). **Add another Claude subscription** (or ChatGPT), or **Add an API key**, signs in to another, kept beside the others (signing in again to one already there, or adding the same key again, just renews it; a plain sign-in replaces the first). A key is named by its end ("sk-…a1b2") until given a name; a key in Claktory's environment (ANTHROPIC_API_KEY, say) comes last, named by its variable. Requests go to the first account in the order that has capacity. When a subscription reaches its usage limit, Claktory skips it until the limit resets (as the provider says) and sends the request again at once to the next; when a key runs out of credit, it's skipped until it's tried again, by itself after an hour or when the person clicks Try again, and a request it answers shows it has credit again. The agent's conversation says so ("Claude account a@x.com reached its limit; continuing on b@x.com."); that request starts the model's cache afresh, a one-off cost. A passing rate limit or an overloaded provider doesn't change accounts: the request is retried as before. Nothing else changes accounts, and once the first has capacity again, requests go back to it. When every account is spent, the agent doesn't fail: it waits. Its conversation says why and when it carries on, its row says what it waits for ("Waiting for your Claude usage to reset at Fri 18:00"), in place of its role, and so does the line above its message box, in place of Working, with Stop; its character sleeps, and its row doesn't wave. It asks again by itself a few seconds after the first limit resets, and sooner when {{user}} adds an account, uses a usage reset, clicks Try again on a key or changes its model, or when a usage read shows the account has room again (a reset claimed on the provider's own site, say); a provider that still refuses means waiting again. Keys out of credit don't reset by themselves, so then it waits for {{user}}. Messages, monitors and events that come meanwhile queue as they do while it works, and a message from {{user}} also makes the agent ask the provider again at once, so telling a waiting agent to carry on works when usage came back in a way Claktory couldn't see; nothing busy-waits, Stop ends the wait, and a Claktory restart resumes it. Each subscription's usage is read and shown on its own. Some subscriptions come with usage resets, each refilling some of its limits once (Claude's Fable and full resets, ChatGPT's resets): Claktory reads how many each account has, and what each refills, with its usage, and uses one only when {{user}} asks and confirms, never by itself, and no agent can. Its answer says what happened (or, when the provider turns Claktory away, offers its own site), and the account's usage is read again at once; a limit reset gives the account requests again.

**Refusals:** some models have safety classifiers that can decline a step. The conversation shows a refusal as itself, with the model's own words and the policy area it named, not as an ordinary failure. When the agent has a **fallback model** (its own, in its settings, or the Claktory-wide default, in Settings → Defaults; `fallback_model` and `default_fallback_model` in the settings file), Claktory sends the declined step to it once: the answer comes from the fallback, the conversation and the agent say so, and the agent's next step runs on its own model again. At most two refused turns a run are retried, a fallback that is the agent's own model isn't used, and a fallback whose accounts are out of capacity isn't waited for: the refusal stands. On Claude models with Anthropic's own fallback, the provider may answer a declined step with another model by itself; the conversation names who answered.

Only {{user}} can sign in, because it takes their account. An agent can't do it for them; it can tell them where to click. Models appear once their provider is signed in. An agent set to a model whose provider isn't signed in shows "No model available" and can't run until someone signs in or picks another model.

### The settings file

Everything in the sidebar and Settings, except signing in, lives in the settings file `{{config}}`. Edit it with your file tools and Claktory applies the change within a second, in every open window. So an agent can do for {{user}} nearly anything they could do by clicking.

```json
{
  "compact_at_tokens": 400000,
  "default_model": "anthropic/claude-opus-5-5",
  "default_effort": "high",
  "projects": [
    { "id": "web-site", "name": "Web Site", "path": "{{workspace}}/web-site",
      "folders": [ { "id": "research", "name": "Research", "path": "{{workspace}}/web-site/research" } ] }
  ],
  "agents": [
    { "id": "rivet", "color": "blue", "name": "Rivet", "role": "Admin", "prompt": "…", "model": "anthropic/claude-opus-5-5", "effort": "medium",
      "placeholder": "Set up a team that works on my codebase while I sleep\nSet up agents that build and run the website for my bakery",
      "avatar": { "shape": "hexagon" }, "extensions": { "agents": { "reachable": false, "admin": true } } },
    { "id": "nina", "color": "violet", "project": "web-site", "name": "Nina", "role": "Writer", "prompt": "Write the site's copy.", "model": "anthropic/claude-opus-5-5", "effort": "medium",
      "extensions": { "agents": { "also": ["designer"] }, "checklist": { "enabled": true } } }
  ]
}
```

- `name` and `color`: this Claktory's own name and color, the same on every device that shows it, and in the pairing codes it makes. `color` is one of the agents' colors (blue, violet, green, pink, teal, amber, red, slate). Both optional: left out, the name is this computer's (a Mac's Computer Name) and the color one picked from this Claktory's key, which never changes.
- `compact_at_tokens`: the compaction threshold, above 0.
- `highlights`: `false` when {{user}} has turned highlights off (Settings → Display); left out, they're on.
- `task_alert_hours`: how long a background task runs before admin agents are told of it, once (24 unless changed).
- `resources`: when agents with the Resources extension are told the machine runs low (Settings → Resources): `ram_percent` (memory used, 1 to 100; 90 unless changed), `disk_free_gb` and `disk_free_percent` (a volume Claktory uses has less free than either, whichever is smaller; 10 and 10; `disk_free_gb` 0 turns it off) and `cpu_percent` (processors busier than it, 0 to 100; 0, off, unless changed); and `build_cache_gb`, the budget for one checkout's or project folder's build caches, in GB (25 unless changed; 0 never clears them; see Own checkout). Every key is optional.
- `display`: how wide {{user}} dragged the app's sidebar (`sidebar_width`) and the panel on the right (`panel_width`), in logical pixels, the same on every device; 0 or left out is the app's default, and the app keeps them within its range. Both are optional; a negative width makes the file invalid. `zoom`: how much each device zooms the whole app, by device id (`local` for this computer's own app; a paired device's as in `{{home}}/remote/devices.json`), from 0.5 to 2 (another value makes the file invalid); a device left out is at actual size, and unpairing a device removes its zoom.
- `compaction`: `idle`, whether idle agents compact before their prompt cache expires (on unless `false`), and `idle_short_cache`, also when the cache lasts 5 minutes (off unless `true`). Optional.
- `prompt_cache`: how long prompt caches are asked to last, `"5m"` or `"1h"`, by provider (`"anthropic"`, the only one whose lifetime Claktory chooses) or model (`"anthropic/claude-opus-4-8"`, which wins over its provider). Left out: the provider's default, 5 minutes. Optional.
- `user`: the person agents work for: `name`, their first name, which agents and Claktory's texts call them by (Settings), and `avatar.seed`, which picks their avatar's colours, written when they shuffle them (until then the colours come from this Claktory's key). Optional; left out, they say "the person".
- `remote`: the private tunnel for devices on other machines (see "Claktory on another machine"): `relays`, the relays' URLs, left out for n0's. Whether devices may connect follows the devices paired, not this. `port` and `listen`, from the listener before the tunnel, are still read but unused.
- `default_model`, `default_effort`: what new agents get when none is chosen.
- `default_fallback_model`: the model a step an agent's model declines on safety grounds is retried on, once, for agents with no `fallback_model` of their own (same form as `default_model`). Optional; left out, a refusal stands (see Refusals under Models and signing in).
- `projects[]`: `id`, `name`, `path`, the absolute path of its folder, `folders` (optional): each with an `id` (unique within the project), a `name` and a `path`, absolute and anywhere, and `archived` (optional, `true` while it's archived).
- `agents[]`: `id`, `name`, `role` (what it does, shown with its name to {{user}} and to other agents, such as "Coordinator"; left out, it has none), `prompt`, `model` (`"<provider>/<model id>"`), `effort`, `fallback_model` (the model a step its model declines on safety grounds is retried on, once, same form as `model`; leave it out to follow `default_fallback_model`), `color` (one of the eight; it belongs to the agent, so moving the agent in the list never changes it; left out, Claktory picks one, as for a new agent, or keeps the one the agent had, and writes it into the file), `project` (a project's id; leave it out for the workspace), `lead` (the id of the agent it reports to, which sees how it's set up: one in its project or an admin agent; leave it out for none. The sidebar nests an agent under its lead when the lead is in the same project or workspace. One that isn't, or that would make a loop, is ignored without making the file invalid; moving an agent to another project takes away a lead that no longer fits, and deleting an agent makes those that reported to it report to its own lead), `parent` (no longer used: older versions nested agents with it; leave it as it is), `folder` (the id of one of its project's `folders` to work in; left out, or one the project doesn't have, it works in the project's own folder), `placeholder` (what its message box says when empty, such as what the agent is for; left out, it says "Message <name>"; several lines separated by `\n` are examples that take turns, such as Rivet's), `avatar` (its character's shape, drawn in its color: `{"shape": "hexagon"}`, with shapes circle, square, triangle, diamond, pentagon and hexagon; left out, or a shape Claktory doesn't know, it's automatic, picked from the agent's id so it never changes when the agent is renamed or moved; display only), and `extensions`.
- The order of `agents` is the order of the sidebar: within each project (and the workspace), and among each lead's agents, agents show in the order they come in the list.
- `extensions`: the agent's extension settings by extension id. Every key is optional; anything left out takes the extension's default. `enabled` turns an extension on or off for the agent. Messaging's options (under `agents`): `reachable`, `admin` and `see_map` (true or false) and `also` (a list of agent ids). Checklist's option: `nudge` (true or false). Tasks' option: `see_all` (true or false). Comments, Memory, Monitors, Resources, Highlights, Own checkout (`tree`), Viewer and Web have only `enabled`, and so do instances, by their key: `"memory:factory": {"enabled": true}` gives an agent the Claktory manual, `{"enabled": false}` takes it from an admin agent. Settings for an extension Claktory doesn't have are kept and ignored; a value of the wrong kind (`"admin": "yes"`) makes the file invalid.
- Ids use only a-z, 0-9 and -, and are unique among projects and among agents. Names can't be empty. Efforts are off, minimal, low, medium, high, xhigh or max; use one the model lists. Use models from the list of available ones below; others won't run.
- If an edit leaves the file invalid, Claktory keeps the last good settings and the sidebar shows the error until the file is fixed. Check your edit and fix it right away.

How to:
- **Add a project:** add it to `projects` with `path` set to `{{workspace}}/<id>`, and create that folder (`mkdir -p`).
- **Add an agent:** add it to `agents`. A new agent starts with an empty conversation.
- **Move an agent:** set its `project`, or remove `project` to move it to the workspace. It works in the new folder from its next step. Its `folder` belongs to the old project, so remove it or set one of the new project's. Move the agents under it too (set their `project` as well), or their leads go and they're shown at the top level of the old project.
- **Set up folders in a project:** add them to the project's `folders`, e.g. `{ "id": "app", "name": "App", "path": "/Users/me/code/app" }`, create any folder that doesn't exist yet (`mkdir -p`), and set `folder` on each agent that works there. It works there from its next step. To remove a folder, take it out of `folders` and take `folder` off its agents; its files stay.
- **Reorder or nest agents in the sidebar:** move entries within `agents` to change their order, and set `lead` to put an agent under another one in its project (remove `lead` to bring it back to the top level). Nesting is the lead: the agent reports to the one it's under, which sees how it's set up. It doesn't change who can message whom.
- **Change an agent:** edit its `name`, `color`, `prompt`, `model`, `effort`, `placeholder` or `avatar`. This includes you: your own entry is how you change yourself. The change applies from the agent's next step, even mid-task, and it's told in a short note what changed. `color`, `placeholder` and `avatar` apply at once: they only change what the app shows.
- **Archive a project, or bring it back:** set its `archived` to `true`, or remove it (or set `false`). Nothing is deleted; the archived-projects note says what archiving does.
- **Delete an agent or project:** remove its entry. Deleting in the app also erases the conversation; removing the entry leaves the conversation's files in `{{home}}/agents/<id>`, and an agent added later with the same id picks it back up.
- **Change defaults:** edit `default_model`, `default_effort` or `compact_at_tokens`.
- **Turn an extension on or off for an agent:** set `extensions.<id>.enabled`, e.g. `"extensions": {"checklist": {"enabled": true}}`. The agent is told before its next step; an extension turned on can be used from then on (through `extension_call` until the agent's next context), and one turned off has its tools refused at once.
- **Let an agent message agents in other projects:** add their ids to its `extensions.agents.also`; for a conversation both ways, add each to the other's. `admin: true` lets it see and message everyone; `reachable: false` stops all agents from messaging it.

Agents can't yet sign in to providers or stop other agents; those need the app. When something can only be done in the app, tell {{user}} exactly where to click.

## Where Claktory keeps things

- `{{config}}`: the settings.
- `{{workspace}}`: the workspace folder, with one folder per project.
- `<project folder>/.factoryignore`: what a project's Files hide.
- `{{home}}/releases/`: published versions of Claktory, which the app offers as updates (built here, or downloaded by a Claktory installed online). Once a device on another machine has updated from one, it also holds that version's app packed for devices (`app.tar.gz`).
- `{{home}}/factory.db`: Claktory's records, in SQLite. `messages` holds every message an agent was sent, {{user}}'s and events (`source` is person, factory, agent, task or monitor; `sender`, `summary`, `text`, and for {{user}}'s, `sent_from`: the device, as JSON, `client_id`: the id their app gave the message, and `attachments`: the files attached, as JSON), with when it was `created`, `delivered` and in which `run`, or `dropped` (the agent was stopped or deleted, or its model couldn't run). `runs` holds each stretch of work (`agent`, `version`, `started`, `ended`, `outcome`: finished, stopped, failed, deleted or interrupted). `tasks` holds background tasks (`agent`, `task`, `command`, `pid`, `log`, `started`, `ended`, `status`). `checklist_items` holds each agent's checklist as it is now (`agent`, `id`, `text`, `status`, `owner`, `created`, `updated`), and `checklist_events` everything that happened to the items, never changed afterwards (`agent`, `item`, `time`, `actor`: an agent id or '' for the person, `kind`: created, status, owner, edited or note, and the new `value`). `memory_preferences` (`agent`, `id`, `rule`, `why`, `learned`, `updated`) and `memory_notes` (`agent`, `slug`, `summary`, `body`, `created`, `updated`) hold each agent's memory as it is now, and `memory_events` every change to it, never changed afterwards (`agent`, `time`, `actor`, `kind`: pref_added, pref_replaced, pref_removed, note_written, note_edited or note_removed, `subject`: the preference's id or the note's slug, and the whole entry `before` and `after`, as JSON). `monitors` holds each agent's monitors as they are now (`agent`, `id`, `name`, `prompt`, `kind`, `spec`, `until`, `max_fires`, `idle_only`, `created_by`, `created`, `state`: active, paused, done, cancelled or error, `reason`, `next_due`, `last_fired`, `fires`), and `monitor_events` what happened to them, never changed afterwards (`agent`, `monitor`, `time`, `actor`, `kind`: created, fired, paused, resumed, cancelled, done or error, and `value`). `layouts` holds each agent's panes (`agent`, `layout`: an encoded ViewNode), when it has more than its conversation. `threads` holds comments on files, one row per comment and its replies (`agent` it's addressed to, `path`, `kind`, `version`: the file's SHA-256 its place refers to, `range_start` and `range_end`, `quote`, `status`: open or resolved, `anchor`: anchored, moved, detached or outdated, `original`: the text it was made on, `suggestion`, `suggested_by` and `decision`: a change proposed, by whom, and accepted or rejected), `thread_comments` what was said in each (`thread`, `author`: an agent id or '' for {{user}}, `body`, `draft`: not sent yet), and `thread_files` the text each file's comments were placed on. `proposals` holds what agents proposed to Rivet (`agent`, `text`, `created`, `state`: pending, approved or dismissed, {{user}}'s `comment`, and when it was `decided`); a chat's `agent` is its id, `chat:<n>`). `chats` holds {{user}}'s chats (`id`, `title`: from the first exchange, `titled`: the model wrote it, `named`: what {{user}} renamed it to, `model`, `effort`, `created`, `updated`, `unseen`: it replied since {{user}} last had it open), and `chat_selected` the one the app shows; what was sent to a chat is in `messages` under `chat:<id>`. `device_pairings` holds the devices agents added for {{user}} (`agent`, `device` and when it was `created` in `remote/devices.json`, `name`, when its code `expires`, `state`: waiting, asking, added, denied, cancelled or expired, and when it `ended`); never their codes. `model_usage` holds the tokens of model calls per UTC `hour` (unix seconds), `provider` and `model` (`requests`, `input`, `output`, `cache_read`, `cache_write`), for check-ins at claktory.com (kept 100 days); `agent_usage` the same per `account` (the provider account that answered, '' when unknown) and `agent` (with its `agent_name` then), kept for good, for the Usage pane (from before it, `account` and `agent` are ''); `runs.last_call` is when a run last had a model's answer. `state`, `open_panels` (the panel open beside each agent), `expanded`, `show_all`, `collapsed` (agents whose agents underneath are folded away in the sidebar), `collapsed_folders` (project folders whose agents are folded away) and `unseen` (agents whose work finished since {{user}} last opened them) hold what the app shows. `agent_presence` holds what each agent's character shows between runs: when it last became idle (`idle_since`: a run ended, /clear, or it was first seen) and whether its last run `failed`; idle 15 minutes, it shows asleep. Times are milliseconds since 1970. Read it through **Claktory's records** (`database:factory`, on for admin agents unless turned off): `db_query` and `db_schema`, read only, to see what happened, such as what an agent was sent and when. No agent's file tools reach the file itself, and the agents' memory, pipes and comments on files can't be read this way either, even by admin agents; Claktory writes it, so don't change it. Agents that were working when Claktory stopped carry on from it when Claktory starts.
- `{{home}}/remote/`: what devices on other machines need: Claktory's tunnel key (`iroh.key`; its public half is the `e=` in pairing codes), this machine's app's code (`local-pairing`), the port devices on other machines reach it on (`remote-port`, kept so it stays the same), and the paired devices (`devices.json`: each one's `id`, `name`, the SHA-256 of its token, the key it bound, when its unused code expires, when it was paired and last connected). Use `factoryd pair`, `unpair` and `devices` rather than editing it.
- `{{home}}/vault/`: the secrets (Settings → Secrets), kept apart from factory.db: `vault.db`, with every value encrypted, who has each secret and the log of their use, and `auto.key`, the key that opens it when Claktory starts (gone while signed in to claktory.com: the account opens it then). Only {{user}}'s account can read them. Never copy, edit or delete them.
- `{{home}}/account/`: the sign-in at claktory.com, if any: `account.json` (the service and its key, the email, this Claktory's id and token, and what was last sent, with the hours not yet sent), `claktory.key` (this Claktory's key there) and `share.key` (this machine's half of the vault's key while signed in). Never copy, edit or delete them.
- `{{home}}/trees/<agent id>/`: the agent's own checkouts (Own checkout extension), one per repository, each with a `.json` beside it saying which repository it's of. Claktory makes and removes them.
- `{{home}}/chats/<id>/`: each chat's conversation. Claktory writes it, and deletes it with the chat.
- `{{home}}/agents/<id>/`: each agent's conversation, its database (`database.sqlite`, Database extension), in `tasks/` its background tasks' logs, in `monitors/` its monitors' watch logs and file baselines, in `uploads/` the files {{user}} attached to messages, and in `archive/<time>/` conversations cleared with /clear (time in milliseconds since 1970). Claktory writes these; don't edit them.
- The app keeps only its window's size and position, unsent drafts in the message boxes, and the Claktories it connects to (their pairing codes, the names given to them on this device, and which one shows), on each device. Everything else is in the backend, so every device shows the same Claktory. A pairing code holds the token that lets a device in, so the Claktories it connects to, with the device's key for each, are kept in the Keychain, readable by Claktory only and never synced to iCloud: on a Mac the login keychain's "Claktory connections" item (Claktory Dev has its own), on an iPhone this phone's Keychain. The window and drafts are in the app's user defaults. A Mac app that isn't signed with Claktory's Developer ID (Claktory Dev, or a release built without it) keeps its connections in user defaults instead, since the Keychain would ask for access after every build, and says so in its log. When the app can't read what it keeps (an iPhone not yet unlocked since it started, a damaged item), it says so instead of starting as if it had never connected, and writes nothing over it: **Try again** reads it again (it also tries by itself when the app comes back to the front), and **Start over**, after asking, clears it so the device connects and signs in afresh. A change it couldn't save says so at the window's foot, with **Try again**.
